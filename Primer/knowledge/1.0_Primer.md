## Purpose

The purpose of this primer is to provide an introduction to the Treasury Investors Portal. It explains the purpose and operation of some of the apps, as well as the related terminology and concepts. It covers the portal home page, then some of the apps.

## The Portal Home Page

The portal home page shows the apps grouped by category, and a card at the bottom with the Mission statement and links to the Project Vision and the Knowledge Map. The Mission statement explains the purpose of the portal:

> Provide web apps for analyzing, managing, and understanding investments in U.S. Treasury securities, with an emphasis on TIPS.

In the Mission statement and on the portal home page, the terms *Treasury securities* and *TIPS* appear, so these are defined first. Wherever applicable, definitions come from the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), with a citation to the applicable CFR part.

**Marketable security.** "*Marketable security* means a security that may be bought, sold and transferred in the secondary market" (§356.2). This definition comes from Part 356 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), which covers only Treasury securities, so *Treasury* is implied. Taken on its own, "a security that may be bought, sold and transferred in the secondary market" could just as easily describe a share of stock. *Secondary market* means you can buy and sell the security through a brokerage firm, like Schwab, Fidelity, or Vanguard (the "big three").

That citation, §356.2, is this primer's first reference to the CFR. Every citation like it points to a location in this hierarchy:

| Level | Name | Part / Section |
|---|---|---|
| Title 31 | Money and Finance: Treasury | — |
| Subtitle B | Regulations Relating to Money and Finance | 51–1099 |
| Chapter II | Fiscal Service, Department of the Treasury | 200–399 |
| Subchapter A | Bureau of the Fiscal Service | 200–399 |
| Part 356 | Sale and Issue of Marketable Book-Entry Treasury Bills, Notes, and Bonds (Department of the Treasury Circular, Fiscal Service Series No. 1-93) | 356.0–356.35 |
| Subpart A | General Information | 356.0–356.5 |

§356.2 is the second section of Subpart A. This primer also cites Subparts B, C, and D of Part 356, and Appendix B and Appendix C to Part 356, each explained the same way the first time it comes up.

**Treasury.** The word *Treasury* is used several ways in this primer and in the apps: as shorthand for the Treasury Department, the government agency; as shorthand for all marketable Treasury securities; or as shorthand for just the non-TIPS marketable securities. The CFR itself never uses bare *Treasury* for the securities, always *Treasury security* or *Treasury securities*. This primer uses *Treasury Department* for the agency, and *Treasury* alone only when the meaning is clear from context.

**Treasuries and TIPS.** Marketable Treasury securities include both securities indexed to inflation via the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and non-indexed securities. TIPS, Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities, are the indexed ones (Appendix B, §I.B of Part 356). Several of the apps have separate tabs for Treasuries and TIPS, where Treasuries means the non-TIPS securities; this primer follows the same usage.

Per TreasuryDirect, the United States Treasury offers five types of Treasury marketable securities: Treasury Bills, Treasury Notes, Treasury Bonds, TIPS, and Floating Rate Notes (FRNs).

| Type | Maturities |
|---|---|
| Treasury Bills | 4 weeks to 52 weeks |
| Treasury Notes | 2, 3, 5, 7, and 10 years |
| Treasury Bonds | 20 and 30 years |
| TIPS | 5, 10, and 30 years |

Notes, Bonds, and TIPS pay interest every six months. Bills are sold at face value or at a discount from face value; the interest is the difference between the purchase price and the face value paid at maturity. FRNs pay interest quarterly and are indexed to the discount rate on 13-week Treasury bills rather than the CPI. This primer does not cover FRNs further: although they can be bought at auction, brokerages generally do not offer them on the secondary market, so they are difficult to sell and of little use to retail investors.

**The Knowledge Map.** The portal's home page also links to a Knowledge Map: a diagram of every app plus the underlying spec/knowledge docs behind each one, including a Foundations column (Bond Basics, TIPS Basics, CUSIP Ref, Bond Ladders, TIPS Ladders, Rebalancing, SA Logic). Working through the knowledge map could be a useful educational experience if you are more technically savvy.

## Portal Structure

The apps are grouped into four categories: Daily Workflow, Reference, Educational, and External Resources. Below are brief summaries of the apps and the terminology they introduce.

**Daily Workflow**

- *Yields Monitor.* Live intraday and historical yield charts for selected maturities of TIPS and Treasuries. "*Yield* means the annualized rate of return to maturity on a non-indexed security. Yield is expressed as a percentage. For an inflation-protected security, yield means the real yield. Yield is also referred to as 'yield to maturity.'" (§356.2, Yield). Full treatment, including a cross-reference to the CFA Institute's YTM definition, is in the Yields Monitor chapter (YTM = Yield To Maturity, which is implied if *yield* is used without a qualifier.)
- *Yield Curves.* Treasury yield curves for TIPS and Treasuries, including seasonally-adjusted (SA) and SA with outlier adjustment (SAO) series for TIPS. A yield curve shows yield plotted against maturity date or term to maturity.
- *Ladder Manager.* Build and rebalance TIPS ladders, with deep drill traceability from ladder totals down to the CFR reference. Per the Knowledge Map's Bond Ladders reference, a TIPS ladder is built to "construct a portfolio of securities with staggered maturities that produces a consistent cash flow at regular intervals," where "each rung = one calendar year."

**Reference**

- *Treasury Auctions.* Upcoming and historical Treasury auction results, with configurable columns and spreadsheet-style filtering across all security types. "*Auction* means a bidding process by which we sell marketable Treasury securities to the public" (§356.2, Auction). This is the first CFR quote to use the term *we*, which the CFR defines: "*We* (or 'us') means the Secretary of the Treasury and his or her delegates, including the Department of the Treasury, Bureau of the Fiscal Service, and their representatives" (§356.2, We).
- *TIPS Reference.* A live TIPS reference table with sorting, filtering, and CSV export. Its columns include CUSIP (the unique identifying number assigned to each security, per §356.2), maturity, coupon, dated date, dated date ref CPI, price, index ratio, adjusted price, and yield.
- *CPI Explorer.* U.S. CPI history from 1913 and daily Reference CPI (Ref CPI) history from 1998: index levels, year-over-year and month-over-month percent changes, rolling windows, and point-to-point annualized change over any date range. "*Consumer Price Index* (CPI) means the monthly non-seasonally adjusted U.S. City Average All Items Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics of the Department of Labor. We use the CPI as the basis for adjusting the principal amounts of inflation-protected securities" (§356.2, Consumer Price Index), where *we* again means the Treasury Department, as defined earlier.
- *Taxation of Treasuries.* Federal and state tax treatment of Treasury bills, notes, bonds, and TIPS: Original Issue Discount (OID), inflation adjustments, 1099 reconciliation, and state exemption reference docs.
- *Fund Holdings.* Shows the holdings of several Treasury and TIPS exchange-traded funds (ETFs), one tab per fund. Holdings are enriched with yield, term, and duration, plus fund-level weighted averages.

**Educational**

- *Portal Primer* (this app).
- *Seasonal Adjustments.* An app intended to help you understand why seasonal adjustments are necessary for TIPS, and how these adjustments are calculated. SA (Seasonally Adjusted) is the adjustment that factors the seasonal component out of a TIPS yield, giving a more useful number for investment decisions than the raw quoted yield.

**External Resources**

- *After-Tax TIPS Ladder Calculator.* A hosted, independently-maintained web application for calculating after-tax returns on TIPS ladders. Third-party, not a portal app, listed as a complement to the suite.

## Yields Monitor

Yields Monitor is the first app in the Daily Workflow category, and the first one covered in full here.

**Time Series tab (the landing view).** On screen:
- Date-range buttons (2D/10D/1Y/2Y/3Y/10Y/ALL/Custom)
- A TIPS checklist (1/2/5/10/30-Year) and a Treasuries checklist (1-Mth through 30-Year, 9 maturities), each row showing a live yield percentage and a day-change percentage
- Toggle checkboxes (Sync Zoom & Pan, Lock Right, Show SA Yield (1Y/2Y/5Y TIPS))
- A TIPS Yields row of charts and a Treasury Yields row of charts, one chart per checked maturity

*Yield* was introduced in the Portal Structure section. Its full CFR definition: "*Yield* means the annualized rate of return to maturity on a non-indexed security. Yield is expressed as a percentage. For an inflation-protected security, yield means the real yield. Yield is also referred to as 'yield to maturity.'" (§356.2, Yield). This CFR definition isn't how yield is defined in financial theory, though it's good enough for this primer's purposes: "The YTM is the single interest rate that equates the present value of future cash flows to the price of the bond" ([CFA Institute](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/fixed-income-bond-valuation-prices-and-yields)).

*What each chart shows.* Each chart shows the yield of a specific, actual Treasury security over time, not a synthetic constant-maturity index (a calculated value for an exact term, such as exactly 10 years, that no single security has). From the app's footer: "Yields for each maturity are bid yields for the most recently issued Treasury of that maturity." As newer securities are auctioned, the security a chart tracks changes from time to time; the data provider does not document exactly when.

*Bid yield.* A market quote has two sides: the *bid*, the price you receive if you sell, and the *ask*, the price you pay if you buy. Treasuries are quoted in both price terms and yield terms, and either form can be calculated from the other. The *bid yield* corresponds to the bid price, and the *ask yield* to the ask price. The yields in this app are bid yields; the difference between the two sides, the *bid-ask spread*, is covered in the Yield Curves chapter.

*Day-change units.* The change percentage next to each yield is a percentage-point change in yield, not a relative percent change: a move from 4.551% to 4.547% displays as "−0.004%."

*The "Show SA Yield (1Y/2Y/5Y TIPS)" checkbox.* TIPS yields, especially at shorter maturities, are subject to predictable seasonal patterns that make the raw quoted yield a weaker gauge of investment value. *SA* (seasonally adjusted) yields correct for that pattern. This checkbox overlays the SA yield, in amber, on the 1-Year, 2-Year, and 5-Year TIPS charts; the seasonal effect shrinks as maturity grows and is negligible at 10 and 30 years, so no overlay is offered there. The mechanics are covered in the Yield Curves chapter.

*Faded chart segments.* The line fades to about a third opacity outside the 08:00–17:00 Eastern Time (ET) trading session, on 2D/10D ranges only, marking after-hours and weekend segments as distinct from the regular session.

*Sync Zoom & Pan and Lock Right.* With Sync Zoom & Pan checked (the default), zooming or panning any chart applies the same time window to all the other charts, for comparing yield movements across maturities. With Lock Right checked, the right edge of a chart stays pinned to the latest data while you zoom.

**Yield Curves tab.** On screen: two curve charts (TIPS curve, Treasury curve), each plotting a dashed line for the start of the selected date range and a solid line for the end. A *yield curve* was touched on in the Portal Structure section; this tab shows the first chart of one, and the Yield Curves app, covered in its own chapter, gives the full treatment. This tab ignores the maturity checklists: it always plots all 5 TIPS and all 9 Treasury maturities, regardless of what is checked in the sidebar. The Show SA Yield checkbox does apply here, adding amber SA points at the 1-Year, 2-Year, and 5-Year TIPS maturities. Each chart's legend lists an "(SA)" entry even while the checkbox is off; the SA line itself is drawn only while the checkbox is on.

**Breakeven Inflation tab.** *Breakeven Inflation* (BEI) equals Treasury yield minus TIPS yield, for the same maturity. It is a useful proxy for the market's expected inflation, though there are technical reasons it can deviate somewhat from actual inflation expectations. The chart works like the Yield Curves tab charts: a dashed line for the range start, a solid line for the range end, all maturities always plotted, and an amber SA overlay at the 1-, 2-, and 5-year maturities while the checkbox is on.

## Yield Curves

The Yield Curves app plots the TIPS yield curve and the Treasury yield curve, one point per security. These curves are much more complete than the ones on the Yields Monitor Yield Curves tab, because they include yields for all of the Treasuries and TIPS available on the secondary market.

**The two tabs.** The app has a TIPS tab (the landing view) and a Treasuries tab, and both work the same way. Each has:
- FedInvest and Market source checkboxes, showing an as-of date and a security count for each source
- A second pair of tabs selecting the view (Yield Curves, Bid-Ask Spreads)
- A maturity RANGE date-picker pair, a horizontal-axis choice (Maturity, Term), and a Reset Zoom button
- The curve chart
- A table of the plotted securities

*Maturity or Term.* The horizontal-axis choice selects between the two forms of a yield curve introduced in the Portal Structure section: Maturity plots each security at its maturity date, and Term plots it at its term to maturity, the time remaining until that date.

*Viewing a maturity range.* The RANGE fields, zooming, and panning all change which range of the curve is shown. In the RANGE fields, type a start and end date with or without slashes, or click to use the calendar control. The bottom of the chart card lists the zoom and pan controls (Zoom: Wheel / +/- keys; Pan: Drag / Arrows). Reset Zoom returns the chart to the default zoom level, undoing any dates entered and any zooming or panning.

*FedInvest and Market.* These are the two price sources. FedInvest is TreasuryDirect's daily price data for Treasury securities; these prices apply to trades made directly between governmental agencies, not to trades on the secondary market. The yields shown for this source are derived from the FedInvest prices; they are not representative of market yields, which you can see by comparing the FedInvest and Market curves. FedInvest is offered because it is available to anybody and is used by non-portal apps like tipsladder.com. Market quotes come directly from a broker and are downloaded several times throughout the day; a quote includes both prices and yields, so the yields shown for this source are taken directly from the quotes. Market is selected by default because these are the yields more relevant to secondary market trades. On the chart, FedInvest curves are dotted and Market curves are solid.

**The TIPS tab.** The TIPS table has columns Maturity, CUSIP, Coupon, Price, Ask Yield, SA Yield, SAO Yield, and Diff (bps), and each column header is clickable, showing that column's definition. Show checkboxes (Ask, SA, SAO) above the table toggle the corresponding curves on the chart, and All and None check or uncheck them all at once.

*Ask Yield.* The ask yield is the yield that applies when you buy: you pay the ask price and get the ask yield. When you sell, you receive the bid price, and the bid yield is the yield that applies to that price. Brokers provide both prices and both yields. Ask yields, rather than bid yields, are shown because the portal expects you to buy much more frequently than you sell; when you build a TIPS ladder, for example, you are buying TIPS, so the ask yields are the relevant ones.

*SA Yield.* *SA* stands for seasonally adjusted. TIPS are indexed to the non-seasonally adjusted CPI, and prices for goods like gasoline, clothing, and food rise and fall with the seasons, so the index is subject to predictable seasonal patterns. Because of these seasonal impacts, a TIPS maturing in April can yield more than one maturing in July, even though the yield curve generally slopes upward, with longer maturities yielding more; the effect is largest for TIPS close to maturity. The seasonal adjustment removes this effect, giving a more accurate view of the value of TIPS adjacent in maturity. A later section explains the seasonally adjusted yield more fully.

*SAO Yield.* *SAO* stands for SA with outlier adjustment. Even after the seasonal adjustment, the curve is not smooth: individual SA yields deviate from a smooth curve, and the causes of these deviations are not known (prior analysis has largely ruled out coupon and index ratio). The SAO step fits a smooth fair-value curve through the SA yields and reads each TIPS off that curve, on the working assumption that the deviations do not reflect real differences in value.

*Diff (bps).* *bps* stands for basis points; one basis point (bp) is 0.01 percentage point. Diff is the difference between the SA Yield and the Ask Yield, in basis points, measuring how much the seasonal adjustment moved the yield.

**The Treasuries tab.** The Treasuries table has columns Maturity, CUSIP, Type, Coupon, Price, and Yield. There are no SA, SAO, or Diff columns because only TIPS are indexed to the CPI, so seasonal adjustment does not apply. The Show checkboxes here select security types: Bills, Notes, and Bonds. A Clip Outliers checkbox excludes extreme outlier yields from the vertical axis so the rest of the curve remains readable. With Market data, a STRIPS checkbox adds STRIPS: "*STRIPS* (Separate Trading of Registered Interest and Principal of Securities) means our program under which eligible securities are authorized to be separated into principal and interest components, and transferred separately" (§356.2, STRIPS), where *our* again refers to the Treasury Department, as defined earlier.

**The Bid-Ask Spreads view.** This view requires Market data, since only broker quotes include both sides of the quote; its tab is disabled until the Market source is checked. It shows two panels: Yield Spread (bps), the bid yield minus the ask yield, and Price Spread (% of Ask), the ask price minus the bid price as a percentage of the ask price.

## Ladder Manager

Ladder Manager builds and rebalances a TIPS ladder. As the Portal Structure section noted, quoting the Knowledge Map's Bond Ladders reference, a TIPS ladder is built so "each rung = one calendar year"; in the app, a rung is called a *funded year*. The app has extensive built-in help: a "?" button in the top right opens a full write-up of every concept below, and nearly every number in its tables opens its own calculation-chain popup. This chapter covers what's on screen and points to that help for the full depth, rather than repeating it.

**The two tabs: Rebalance and Build.** Rebalance is the landing view: for an existing ladder, it computes the trades needed to realign current holdings with the ladder's targets. Build designs a ladder from scratch, with no existing holdings. Both share the same underlying math; Rebalance simply starts from a "before" state that Build does not have.

*DARA and ARA.* *DARA* stands for Desired Annual Real Amount: the target amount, in today's dollars, that a funded year is sized to produce. "Real" means inflation-adjusted, as introduced in the Portal Structure section. *ARA* stands for Annual Real Amount: the amount a funded year actually produces, which can differ slightly from DARA because quantities round to whole TIPS.

**The Rebalance tab.** A sample holdings file loads automatically, so you can run a rebalance before uploading anything of your own. On screen:
- A Holdings CSV upload, accepting a direct export from Fidelity, Schwab, or Vanguard, or a simple CUSIP-and-quantity file
- A DARA field, which becomes editable per year after the file loads (see Per-Year DARA, below)
- First Year and Last Year dropdowns, detected from the uploaded holdings and adjustable
- A Brackets selector (2-bracket, 3-bracket), with its own "?" help button
- A Pre-ladder int. checkbox, applying coupon income received before the ladder starts to reduce the earliest funded years' quantities
- A Run Rebalance button

*Bracket years: 2-bracket vs. 3-bracket.* A bracket year holds extra TIPS, beyond what its own funded year needs, to duration-match a block of years where no TIPS are available (see gap years and Future 30-Year rungs, below). *Duration* measures a bond's price sensitivity to a change in yield; matching it means the extra TIPS gain or lose value the same way the missing years would have. The upper bracket is always the Feb 2040 TIPS. For the lower bracket, 2-bracket mode always uses the latest 10-year TIPS issued before the gap (currently Jan 2036), moving any older lower-bracket holdings into ordinary funded years and selling their excess. 3-bracket mode (the default) instead manages the old and new lower-bracket holdings together: it sells from the old one first if there is too much, and never buys more of it if there is too little, so a TIPS originally bought for this role isn't sold off the moment a newer one takes over. The "?" button next to Brackets opens a full comparison of the two modes.

**Reading the results.** The info strip above the table shows the FedInvest price date, the ladder's TIPS count, and the Ref CPI date (click it to override the reference date used for all inflation adjustments). Running Rebalance fills in the term and range, shown as a dash until then, and adds Net Cash, the net proceeds of the rebalance (negative means a net purchase). It also reveals Avg Yld and Avg Mod Dur, the portfolio's weighted average yield and modified duration, and buttons for Export CSV, Export CUSIP/Qty (a file formatted for re-import on a future rebalance), Purchase List, and Gap Dur, which opens the duration-matching calculation behind the bracket weights (a Future 30Y Dur button appears too once the ladder extends past 2056).

**The ladder table.** Each funded year is a row, marked with an asterisk if it is a bracket year (e.g. "2036*"). Clicking a row expands it into its underlying TIPS, showing CUSIP and maturity; a bracket year's extra holdings appear as a labelled sub-row. Expand All and Collapse Bracket/Cover Years buttons above the table do this for every row at once. The columns are Amt Before/After, Cost Before/After, Qty Before/After, Qty Delta, and Cash Delta, comparing current holdings to the rebalance target.

*Gap years and Future 30-Year rungs.* A gap year is a year with no TIPS issued at all, currently 2037 through 2039. A Future 30-Year rung, labelled "(30Y)" in the per-year table, is a year beyond the longest currently issued TIPS, currently 2057 and later. Neither can be held directly, so both are covered by bracket (or, for Future 30-Year rungs, "cover") TIPS at adjacent years, sized so their combined price sensitivity matches the missing years as a group; the app calls this duration matching. *LMI* stands for Later Maturity Interest: coupon income from later-maturing TIPS that reduces how many TIPS an earlier funded year needs to buy. Bracket and cover TIPS extend this by role playing: when they stand in for a maturity that hasn't been issued yet, their coupon counts as LMI too, in place of the coupon the missing TIPS itself would have paid. The app's help gives a worked example.

**Deep drill.** Every underlined number in the table opens a popup showing how it was calculated, down to the individual TIPS and their quantities. Some popups contain further underlined variables, such as the Ref CPI used for a value, that open a second popup breaking that piece down further, and some of those reach a citation to the Code of Federal Regulations.

**Per-Year DARA and the DARA Plan.** After a run, a Per-Year DARA panel lets you override the target amount for any individual funded year rather than using one flat DARA for the whole ladder. A DARA Plan panel above it can split the ladder into segments at a chosen year, for example separating years you plan to spend down from years intended for heirs, and infer a self-financing DARA for each segment separately; it can also remember, export, and import a DARA plan so it survives a fresh holdings upload. Both panels have their own "?" help.

**The Build tab.** Build asks for a DARA, a First Year and Last Year, a Maturity preference (which TIPS fill each funded year: the latest-maturing by default, or the earliest, the January and July TIPS together, or one per distinct maturity month), and a Pre-ladder int. checkbox. Clicking Build Ladder produces a table like Rebalance's, without the Before/After comparison since there is no existing portfolio to compare against.

## Treasury Auctions

Treasury Auctions is the first app in the Reference category. It covers Bills, Notes and Bonds, and TIPS, showing the results of past Treasury auctions, auctions that have been announced but not yet held, and Treasury's tentative future auction schedule.

**The sidebar.** On the left:
- View buttons (All, Bills, Notes & Bonds, TIPS), filtering every tab's table to that security type
- A Columns section: a search box and a checklist of every available column, drag an item to reorder it, check or uncheck it to show or hide it
- Reset Defaults and Clear Filters buttons

**The three tabs.** Historical Auctions is the landing view; Upcoming Auctions and Tentative Schedule sit alongside it.

**Historical Auctions.** The table has two header rows. The top row is the column labels:
- Click one to sort by it
- Drag it to reorder it
- Drag its right edge to resize it
- Hover it and click the "✕" that appears to remove it (the same as unchecking it in the sidebar)

The row below is per-column filters: free text for most columns, or for a date column, a calendar-icon toggle that opens a From/To date-range popover instead. Rows are shaded in alternating bands that group by auction date.

The default columns differ by View:
- Bills: CUSIP, Term, Orig Term, Maturity, Auction Date, Issue Date, High Inv Rate, High Price
- Notes & Bonds: CUSIP, Term, Orig Term, Maturity, Auction Date, Issue Date, High Yield, Coupon, High Price, TIPS
- TIPS: CUSIP, Term, Orig Term, Maturity, Auction Date, Issue Date, Dated Date, High Yield, Coupon, Unadj Price, Index Ratio, Adj Price, Ref CPI (Dated), Ref CPI (Issue)

Any column, from any View, stays available in the Columns checklist regardless of which View is selected.

Two of the columns available in the checklist, though neither is on by default in any View, have their own CFR definitions. *Reopening* (the "Reopen" column) means "the auction of an additional amount of an outstanding security" (§356.2, Reopening). *Bid-to-cover ratio* (the "Bid/Cover" column) means "the total par amount of securities bid for in an auction divided by the total par amount of securities awarded. It excludes bids by, and awards to, the Federal Reserve for its own account." (§356.2, Bid-to-cover ratio)

**Upcoming Auctions.** A plain table, no filters or column picker, pulling live data from Treasury's FiscalData API for auctions announced but not yet held: CUSIP, Announced, Auction Date, Issue Date, Term, Type, Reopen, and TIPS. The TIPS column is set from the security type where the API already labels it, or otherwise by cross-referencing that row's auction date and term against the Tentative Schedule tab, and is shown in green when set.

**Tentative Schedule.** Sourced from Treasury's own tentative auction schedule rather than FiscalData. Its table can be sorted by clicking a column header, but unlike Historical Auctions, columns cannot be dragged, resized, or removed. Columns: Auction Date, Term, Type, Reopen, TIPS, Announcement Date, and Settlement Date.

## TIPS Reference

TIPS Reference is the second app in the Reference category: a read-only table, one row per live TIPS, with no editing, rebalancing, or ladder calculations. As the Portal Structure section noted, its columns include CUSIP (per §356.2, the unique identifying number assigned to each security), maturity, coupon, dated date, dated date ref CPI, price, index ratio, adjusted price, and yield; the table adds several more columns beyond that list. Rows sort by Maturity ascending by default.

**The header.** On screen:
- Price Date, the trade date on which the displayed prices were observed
- Settlement, the next Treasury business day after Price Date (T+1)
- A Ref CPI Date picker, defaulting to Settlement
- Ref CPI, the value looked up for the selected Ref CPI Date
- An Export CSV button

Term, Mac Duration, and Mod Duration are always anchored to Settlement and do not move when the Ref CPI Date picker changes. Index Ratio, Adj Price, Cpn Date, Cpn Date IR, and Cpn $ / TIPS all recompute from whatever date the picker is set to.

*Reference CPI.* "Reference CPI" (Ref CPI) means, for an inflation-protected security, the index number applicable to a given date (§356.2, Reference CPI). A CPI reading is only published once a month, but TIPS math needs a value for every calendar day, so Treasury interpolates one (Appendix B §I.B.3):
- Ref CPI for the first day of a month equals the CPI reported for the third preceding month
- Ref CPI for any other day is found by linear interpolation between the Ref CPI for the first of that month and the first of the next month, truncated to six decimal places and then rounded to five

CFR worked example: Ref CPI for April 1, 1996 (the January 1996 CPI-U) is 154.40; Ref CPI for May 1, 1996 (the February 1996 CPI-U) is 154.90. Ref CPI for April 15, 14 days into a 30-day month, is 154.40 + (14/30) × (154.90 − 154.40) = 154.633333, rounded to 154.63333.

**The table.** CUSIP, Maturity, Coupon, Dated Date, Price, and Yield come straight from the data sources, with no calculation. The rest:
- Dated Date Ref CPI is the security's *base CPI*: Ref CPI as of its dated date, fixed for the life of the bond. *Dated date* means the date from which interest accrues for notes and bonds (§356.2, Dated date)
- Index Ratio and Adj Price
- Term, Mac Duration, and Mod Duration
- Cpn Date, Cpn Date IR, and Cpn $ / TIPS

*Index Ratio and Adj Price.* "Index ratio" means, for an inflation-protected security, the Reference CPI of a particular date divided by the Reference CPI of the original issue date; when the dated date differs from the issue date, the denominator is the Reference CPI of the dated date instead (§356.2, Index ratio). In this table that denominator is the Dated Date Ref CPI column, and the numerator is Ref CPI for whichever date the Ref CPI Date picker is set to. CFR worked example, continuing the security issued April 15, 1996: Index Ratio for April 16, 1996 is 154.65000 / 154.63333 = 1.000107, rounded to 1.00011 (Appendix B §I.B.2–3). Adj Price applies that same ratio to Price: price multiplied by Index Ratio.

*Term and duration.* Term is the time remaining to maturity, the same concept introduced as term to maturity in the Yield Curves chapter; here it is always measured from Settlement. Mac Duration and Mod Duration are the same price-sensitivity-to-yield measures introduced in the Ladder Manager chapter, likewise anchored to Settlement.

*Cpn Date, Cpn Date IR, and Cpn $ / TIPS.* Cpn Date is the coupon payment date on or after the selected Ref CPI Date. Cpn Date IR is the Index Ratio on that date, using the same formula as the Index Ratio column above. Cpn $ / TIPS is the resulting coupon payment: half the annual coupon rate, applied to the inflation-adjusted principal for that date, the par amount times the applicable Index Ratio (Appendix B §I.B.1, §I.B.5). CFR worked example: a 10-year TIPS paying 3⅞% interest, issued January 15, 1999, first interest payment July 15, 1999, base CPI 164, Ref CPI on the payment date 166.2. Inflation-adjusted principal: (166.2 / 164) × $100,000 = $101,341. Payment: $101,341 × (.03875 / 2) = $1,963.48. Cpn $ / TIPS applies that same calculation per $1,000 of face value rather than the $100,000 par amount in this example.

Clicking a filled-in Cpn $ / TIPS cell opens a modal walking through this same calculation for that row: CUSIP, coupon date, coupon rate, semi-annual rate, Index Ratio, the formula in symbolic and numeric form, and the resulting payment.

**Sorting, filtering, and row count.** Click a column header to sort by it, click again to reverse the sort. Below the header is a filter row, working the same way as Treasury Auctions: free text for most columns, or for a date column, a calendar-icon toggle that opens a From/To date-range popover instead. Active filters highlight in amber. A row count below the header reads "N of M TIPS," filtered against total.

**Export CSV.** Exports the currently filtered and sorted rows, matching the on-screen columns and precision, except Cpn $ / TIPS, which exports at 6 decimal places instead of the on-screen 4, so multiplying it by a holding's quantity doesn't lose a cent at large sizes. The filename includes the Settlement date.

## CPI Explorer

CPI Explorer is the third app in the Reference category. It charts CPI history several different ways, over any date range.

**Data Source.** Three checkboxes, any combination selected at once, each drawn as its own line on the chart:
- Monthly CPI-U NSA, checked by default. This is the CPI as defined in the Portal Structure section: "the monthly non-seasonally adjusted U.S. City Average All Items Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics of the Department of Labor. We use the CPI as the basis for adjusting the principal amounts of inflation-protected securities" (§356.2, Consumer Price Index)
- Monthly CPI-U SA, the seasonally adjusted counterpart, stripping the predictable seasonal pattern out of the NSA series to expose the underlying inflation trend. Not part of the CFR's TIPS math; TIPS principal is adjusted from the NSA series
- Daily Ref CPI (1998+), the same daily series covered in the TIPS Reference chapter

**Display Mode.** One radio selection at a time, plus a separate Logarithmic Scale checkbox for the chart's vertical axis:
- Index Level (default): the raw value of each selected series
- Year-over-Year %: percent change versus the same point 12 months earlier
- Month-over-Month %: percent change versus one month earlier
- Rolling Window %: percent change versus a trailing window of N months back, N set below
- Point-to-Point %: total and annualized percent change between two chosen dates

**Rolling Window controls**, shown only in Rolling Window % mode: preset buttons (1Y, 2Y, 5Y, 10Y, 20Y) or a custom months input.

**Date Range**, shown in every mode except Point-to-Point: Start and End date pickers, and a Full History button that resets both to the full extent of the data.

**Point-to-Point controls**, shown only in that mode: Start and End date pickers in place of the Date Range section, with the result displayed as total percent change and annualized change (CAGR, compound annual growth rate).

**The chart.** A Reset Zoom button sits in the header. Zooming and panning work the same way as the other portal charts introduced in the Yields Monitor and Yield Curves chapters. Below the chart, a stats strip reads Current, Period Change, CAGR, Peak, and Trough for whatever is currently visible in the chart's date window.

## Content-driven architecture

`content/deck.md` is the single file that defines both the deck's structure and its text — edit it directly to reorder pages, retitle a chapter, or change any sentence; no JS edit is needed for content changes. `index.html` has zero page-specific text or ordering in it; it fetches and parses this file at load time and builds the stage/TOC/dots from the result.

**Page syntax:**
```
# page-id
ch: Chapter Name
title: Page Title

<blocks...>
```
`page-id` fixes both the page's identity and its position — file order is deck order.

**Block syntax**, inside a page's body:
- `## Term` starts a definition card. If the body underneath contains `### CFR`, `### CFR-list`, or `### Notes` sub-headers (each field's text runs until the next `### ` sub-header or the next block), it renders as a full CFR/Notes review card. With no sub-headers, the heading's body is a simple content card.
- `### CFR-list` mirrors §356.5's own lettered/numbered outline.
- `:::lead`, `:::example`, `:::callout`, `:::aside` — fenced free-text blocks, each rendering as the matching styled element.
- `:::table` — a fenced pipe table.
- `:::card-table Term Name` — a table embedded inside a titled card.
- `:::figure` — an image with a caption.
- `:::timeline` — a dot-row diagram.
- `:::glossary` — a plain bulleted list (reuses card/listcard parsing, no boxed-card styling), used for the Glossary chapter.
- Citations: `[[356.5:§356.5(b)(1)(iv)]]` — the part before `:` is the eCFR URL key (see `CFR_URL` in `deck-manager.js`), the part after is the display label; renders as a linked, parenthesized citation.
- Inline formatting: `__underline__`, `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `` `code` ``. Raw HTML passes through untouched — this is trusted, hand-authored content, not user input.

## Architecture

No build step; ES modules served statically.

| Module | Role |
|--------|------|
| `index.html` | Shell: CSS, topbar/TOC/pager chrome, navigation. Builds `PAGES` from `loadDeck()`; no page-specific business logic. |
| `content/deck.md` | Every page's structure and static content — see "Content-driven architecture" above |
| `src/deck-manager.js` | Parses `content/deck.md` (`loadDeck()`) and renders its blocks to HTML (`renderBlocks()`). The only module that understands the `.md` syntax; holds no page text itself |

### Data source & fallback

Every "live" page needs the R2 fetch to succeed; there is no offline fallback (a "data unavailable" message renders instead). Local dev servers hit CORS when fetching R2 directly from `localhost` — this is expected, not a bug.
