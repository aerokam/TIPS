# 1.0 Treasury Securities — A Primer

## Purpose

This primer teaches the Treasury and TIPS terminology and concepts needed to use the portal apps. It covers the portal home page, then each app in roughly the order it might typically be used, explaining the terms needed to understand what each app's screen shows.

## Content

### The Portal Home Page

The portal home page shows the apps grouped by category, and a card at the bottom with the Mission statement and links to the Project Vision and the Knowledge Map. The Mission statement explains the purpose of the portal:

> Provide web apps for analyzing, managing, and understanding investments in U.S. Treasury securities, with an emphasis on TIPS.

In the Mission statement and on the portal home page, the terms *Treasury securities* and *TIPS* appear, so these are defined first. Wherever applicable, definitions come from the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), with a citation to the applicable CFR part.

**Marketable security.** *Marketable security* means "a security that may be bought, sold and transferred in the secondary market" (§356.2). This definition comes from Part 356 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), which covers only Treasury securities, so *Treasury* is implied. Taken on its own, "a security that may be bought, sold and transferred in the secondary market" could just as easily describe a share of stock. *Secondary market* means you can buy and sell the security through a brokerage firm, like Schwab, Fidelity, or Vanguard (the "big three").

That citation, §356.2, is this primer's first reference to the CFR. Every citation like it points to a location in this hierarchy:

| Level | Name | Part / Section |
|---|---|---|
| Title 31 | Money and Finance: Treasury | — |
| Subtitle B | Regulations Relating to Money and Finance | 51–1099 |
| Chapter II | Fiscal Service, Department of the Treasury | 200–399 |
| Subchapter A | Bureau of the Fiscal Service | 200–399 |
| Part 356 | Sale and Issue of Marketable Book-Entry Treasury Bills, Notes, and Bonds (Department of the Treasury Circular, Fiscal Service Series No. 1-93) | 356.0–356.35 |
| Subpart A | General Information | 356.0–356.5 |

§356.2 is the second section of Subpart A. This primer also cites Subparts B, C, and D of Part 356, and Appendix B and Appendix C to Part 356, each explained the same way the first time it comes up.

**Treasury.** The word *Treasury* is used several ways in this primer and in the apps: as shorthand for the Treasury Department, the government agency; as shorthand for all marketable Treasury securities; or as shorthand for just the non-TIPS marketable securities. The CFR itself never uses bare *Treasury* for the securities, always *Treasury security* or *Treasury securities*. For the agency, the CFR defines *We* (or *us*): "the Secretary of the Treasury and his or her delegates, including the Department of the Treasury, Bureau of the Fiscal Service, and their representatives. The term also includes Federal Reserve Banks acting as fiscal agents of the United States" (§356.2, We). This primer uses *Treasury Department* for the agency, and *Treasury* alone only when the meaning is clear from context.

**Treasuries and TIPS.** Marketable Treasury securities include both securities indexed to inflation via the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and non-indexed securities. TIPS, Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities, are the indexed ones (Appendix B, §I.B of Part 356). Several of the apps have separate tabs for Treasuries and TIPS, where Treasuries means the non-TIPS securities; this primer follows the same usage.

Per TreasuryDirect, the United States Treasury offers five types of Treasury marketable securities: Treasury Bills, Treasury Notes, Treasury Bonds, TIPS, and Floating Rate Notes (FRNs).

| Type | Maturities |
|---|---|
| Treasury Bills | 4 weeks to 52 weeks |
| Treasury Notes | 2, 3, 5, 7, and 10 years |
| Treasury Bonds | 20 and 30 years |
| TIPS | 5, 10, and 30 years |

Notes, Bonds, and TIPS pay interest every six months. Bills are sold at face value or at a discount from face value; the interest is the difference between the purchase price and the face value paid at maturity. FRNs pay interest quarterly and are indexed to the discount rate on 13-week Treasury bills rather than the CPI; this primer does not cover FRNs further, since they aren't traded on the secondary market and are of little use to retail investors.

**The Knowledge Map.** The portal's home page also links to a Knowledge Map: a diagram of every app plus the underlying spec/knowledge docs behind each one, including a Foundations column (Bond Basics, TIPS Basics, CUSIP Ref, Bond Ladders, TIPS Ladders, Rebalancing, SA Logic). Working through the knowledge map could be a useful educational experience if you are more technically savvy.

### Portal Structure

The apps are grouped into four categories: Daily Workflow, Reference, Educational, and External Resources. Below are brief summaries of the apps and the terminology they introduce.

**Daily Workflow**

- *Yields Monitor.* Live intraday and historical yield charts for selected maturities of TIPS and Treasuries. *Yield* means "the annualized rate of return to maturity on a non-indexed security. Yield is expressed as a percentage. For an inflation-protected security, yield means the real yield. Yield is also referred to as 'yield to maturity.'" (§356.2, Yield). Full treatment, including a cross-reference to the CFA Institute's YTM definition, is in the Yields Monitor chapter below (YTM = Yield To Maturity, which is implied if *yield* is used without a qualifier.)
- *Yield Curves.* Treasury yield curves for TIPS and Treasuries, including seasonally-adjusted (SA) and SA with additional outlier-smoothing (SAO) series for TIPS. A yield curve shows yield plotted against maturity date or term to maturity.
- *Ladder Manager.* Build and rebalance TIPS ladders, with deep drill traceability from ladder totals down to the CFR reference. Per the Knowledge Map's Bond Ladders reference, a TIPS ladder is built to "construct a portfolio of securities with staggered maturities that produces a consistent cash flow at regular intervals," where "each rung = one calendar year."

**Reference**

- *Treasury Auctions.* Upcoming and historical Treasury auction results, with configurable columns and spreadsheet-style filtering across all security types. *Auction* means "a bidding process by which we sell marketable Treasury securities to the public" (§356.2, Auction) — *we* here means the Secretary of the Treasury and delegates, including the Treasury Department, as defined above.
- *TIPS Reference.* A live TIPS reference table — CUSIP (a security's unique identifying number, per §356.2), maturity, coupon, dated date, dated date ref CPI, price, index ratio, adjusted price, and yield — with sorting, filtering, and CSV export.
- *CPI Explorer.* U.S. CPI history from 1913 and daily Reference CPI (Ref CPI) history from 1998: index levels, year-over-year and month-over-month percent changes, rolling windows, and point-to-point annualized change over any date range. *Consumer Price Index* (CPI) means "the monthly non-seasonally adjusted U.S. City Average All Items Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics of the Department of Labor. We use the CPI as the basis for adjusting the principal amounts of inflation-protected securities" (§356.2, Consumer Price Index) — *we* again means the Treasury Department, as defined above.
- *Taxation of Treasuries.* Federal and state tax treatment of Treasury bills, notes, bonds, and TIPS — Original Issue Discount (OID), inflation adjustments, 1099 reconciliation, and state exemption reference docs. Not yet decided whether this belongs in the primer's scope.
- *Fund Holdings.* Shows the holdings of several Treasury and TIPS exchange-traded funds (ETFs), enriched with yield, term, and duration, plus fund-level weighted averages — one tab per fund.

**Educational**

- *Portal Primer* (this app).
- *Seasonal Adjustments.* An app intended to help you understand why seasonal adjustments are necessary for TIPS, and how these adjustments are calculated. SA (Seasonally Adjusted) is the adjustment that factors the seasonal component out of a TIPS yield, giving a more useful number for investment decisions than the raw quoted yield.

**External Resources**

- *After-Tax TIPS Ladder Calculator.* A hosted, independently-maintained web application for calculating after-tax returns on TIPS ladders. Third-party, not a portal app, listed as a complement to the suite.

### Yields Monitor

Yields Monitor is the first app in Daily Workflow, and the first one covered in full here.

**Time Series tab (the landing view).** On screen: date-range buttons (2D/10D/1Y/2Y/3Y/10Y/ALL/Custom); a TIPS checklist (1/2/5/10/30-Year) and a Treasuries checklist (1-Mth through 30-Year, 9 maturities), each row showing a live yield percentage and a day-change percentage; toggle checkboxes (Sync Zoom & Pan, Lock Right, Show SA Yield); paired TIPS/Treasury charts for whatever's checked; and a footer disclaimer.

Yield was already introduced on the Portal Structure page above. Here it gets its full CFR definition again, in context: *Yield* means "the annualized rate of return to maturity on a non-indexed security. Yield is expressed as a percentage. For an inflation-protected security, yield means the real yield. Yield is also referred to as 'yield to maturity.'" (§356.2, Yield). So yield and price move inversely; TIPS have a real yield only, Treasuries have a nominal yield only. This CFR definition isn't how yield is defined in financial theory, though it's good enough for this primer's purposes: "The YTM is the single interest rate that equates the present value of future cash flows to the price of the bond" (CFA Institute).

*What "10-Year TIPS Yield" tracks.* One specific, real, currently-outstanding security — whichever was most recently auctioned at that original maturity — not a constructed point exactly 10 years out. It rolls to the newly auctioned security the next time the Treasury Department auctions one of that maturity. Straight from the app's own footer: "Yields for each maturity are bid yields for the most recently issued Treasury of that maturity."

*Bid yield.* The yield computed from a security's bid price, the price you'd receive if you sold. The footer uses this term before the fuller ask/bid pair is needed (Yield Curves' Bid-Ask Spreads mode covers the rest).

*Day-change units.* The change percentage next to each yield is a percentage-point change in yield, not a relative percent change — a move from 4.551% to 4.547% displays as "−0.004%."

*The "Show SA Yield" checkbox.* SA was already glossed on the Portal Structure page above; this is the first time seeing the checkbox that turns it on. Full mechanics (the Seasonal Adjustments app, the S(settle)/S(maturity) ratio) come later.

*Faded chart segments* (minor). The line fades to about a third opacity outside the 08:00–17:00 Eastern Time (ET) trading session, on 2D/10D ranges only — marking after-hours/weekend moves as distinct from the regular session.

Sync Zoom & Pan and Lock Right are chart controls, not Treasury concepts, so they aren't covered here.

**Yield Curves tab.** On screen: two curve charts (TIPS curve, Treasury curve), each plotting a dashed line (start of the selected date range) and a solid line (end). This tab ignores the sidebar checkboxes entirely — it always plots all 5 TIPS and 9 Treasury maturities, regardless of what's checked in the Time Series sidebar. A yield curve was already glossed above; this is the first actual chart of one, a preview only, since full curve-building is covered in the Yield Curves chapter. Both curve charts reserve an "(SA)" legend entry whether or not the sidebar SA checkbox is checked; it only draws a line once checked. The TIPS shown here are real, specific TIPS; the inflation-adjustment mechanics they carry are covered later, in the TIPS Reference chapter.

**Breakeven Inflation tab.** Breakeven Inflation (BEI) equals Treasury yield minus TIPS yield, for the same maturity. It's a decent proxy for the market's expected inflation, though there are technical reasons it can deviate somewhat from actual inflation expectations. Same chart structure as the Yield Curves tab (dashed/solid pair, always-full-curve, always-present "(SA)" legend), applied to BEI per maturity.

**Where these securities come from.** A brief pointer belongs here to the Treasury Auctions app. Part 356 exists under this authority: "Chapter 31 of Title 31 of the United States Code authorizes the Secretary of the Treasury to issue United States obligations, and to offer them for sale with the terms and conditions that the Secretary prescribes" (§356.0). The Treasury Department carries this out through auctions. No auction-mechanics terminology (bid types, stop-out yield) — that stays out of scope.

## Content-driven architecture

`content/deck.md` is the single file that defines both the deck's structure and its text — edit it directly to reorder pages, retitle a chapter, or change any sentence; no JS edit is needed for content changes. `index.html` has zero page-specific text or ordering in it; it fetches and parses this file at load time and builds the stage/TOC/dots from the result.

**Page syntax:**
```
# page-id
ch: Chapter Name
title: Page Title

<blocks...>
```
`page-id` fixes both the page's identity and its position — file order is deck order.

**Block syntax**, inside a page's body:
- `## Term` starts a definition card. If the body underneath contains `### CFR`, `### CFR-list`, or `### Notes` sub-headers (each field's text runs until the next `### ` sub-header or the next block), it renders as a full CFR/Notes review card. With no sub-headers, the heading's body is a simple content card.
- `### CFR-list` mirrors §356.5's own lettered/numbered outline.
- `:::lead`, `:::example`, `:::callout`, `:::aside` — fenced free-text blocks, each rendering as the matching styled element.
- `:::table` — a fenced pipe table.
- `:::card-table Term Name` — a table embedded inside a titled card.
- `:::figure` — an image with a caption.
- `:::timeline` — a dot-row diagram.
- `:::glossary` — a plain bulleted list (reuses card/listcard parsing, no boxed-card styling), used for the Glossary chapter.
- Citations: `[[356.5:§356.5(b)(1)(iv)]]` — the part before `:` is the eCFR URL key (see `CFR_URL` in `deck-manager.js`), the part after is the display label; renders as a linked, parenthesized citation.
- Inline formatting: `__underline__`, `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `` `code` ``. Raw HTML passes through untouched — this is trusted, hand-authored content, not user input.

## Architecture

No build step; ES modules served statically.

| Module | Role |
|--------|------|
| `index.html` | Shell: CSS, topbar/TOC/pager chrome, navigation. Builds `PAGES` from `loadDeck()`; no page-specific business logic. |
| `content/deck.md` | Every page's structure and static content — see "Content-driven architecture" above |
| `src/deck-manager.js` | Parses `content/deck.md` (`loadDeck()`) and renders its blocks to HTML (`renderBlocks()`). The only module that understands the `.md` syntax; holds no page text itself |

### Data source & fallback

Every "live" page needs the R2 fetch to succeed; there is no offline fallback (a "data unavailable" message renders instead). Local dev servers hit CORS when fetching R2 directly from `localhost` — this is expected, not a bug.
