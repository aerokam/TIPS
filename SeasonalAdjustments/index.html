The Rhythm, Measured

SA Factor (daily) CPI-U, 3-month lag

Divide out that rhythm and you get a repeating ratio — the SA Factor — the seasonal signature used to adjust TIPS prices.

SA Factor S over a single calendar year — 5-year average

The SA Factor S = Ref CPI NSA / Ref CPI SA, plotted across a single calendar year. Each point is the 5-year average for that month/day. The trough in late winter (Feb–Mar) and the peak in late summer (Aug–Sep) reflect the predictable seasonal cycle of CPI-U. TIPS with January and April maturities sit near the trough; July and October maturities sit near the peak.

The seasonal gap — and how often it repeats before it matters

The horizontal dashed line is S(settle) — the seasonal factor fixed the day you buy. The wavy line is S(t), the same annual cycle as Chapter 2, stretched across the bond's whole life instead of folded into one year. This example bond pays one coupon a year instead of two (TIPS really pay twice a year, but a second payment date only adds a tiny, mostly-irrelevant correction — see sidebar — so dropping it keeps the picture clean). Every payment then falls on the exact same calendar month/day as maturity, so every payment sits on the identical point of the wave: the vertical bar from the settle line to the wave — the price-adjustment gap between S(settle) and S(paydate) — is the same height every single year. It never "resolves." Bar thickness is that payment's share of the price: thin for ordinary coupons, thick for the final payment (last coupon plus principal — most of the price). The gap itself doesn't shrink at maturity. Maturity just stops being the only payment date that matters in dollars.
