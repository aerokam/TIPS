## TIPS Ladder Terminology

**[DARA (Desired Annual Real Amount)](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#dara):** Target total cash flow for a funded year in real (inflation-adjusted) terms
- Replaces DAA from 2.0 Bond Ladders
- "Real" means in today's dollars

**[ARA (Annual Real Amount)](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ara):** Actual cash flow produced by the ladder for a funded year in real terms
- Replaces AA from 2.0 Bond Ladders
- May differ from DARA due to rounding

**Note on "Real":** All principal and interest values are inflation-adjusted, so "real" is implied throughout

## Maturity Selection Within a Funded Year

A funded year may hold **one or more TIPS** — DARA remains an *annual* target; the year's real P+I
`need` is *split across* whichever TIPS the funded year holds (see §Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split
below). Only [ladder-eligible](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ladder-eligible-tips) TIPS are
candidates — an auctioned-but-unissued TIPS is never selectable, in any mode, by any policy.

`maturityPref` controls which TIPS fill each year, purely from `tipsMap` (no holdings involved —
build and rebalance share the same selection via `selectLadderBonds`). It applies to every funded
year unless the TIPS for a particular year have been picked individually (§Picking Maturities for a
Funded Year):

- **`last`** *(default)* — one TIPS, the latest-maturing in the year. Legacy behavior; byte-for-byte
  unchanged output.
- **`first`** — one TIPS, the earliest-maturing in the year.
- **`semiannual`** — the Jan TIPS + the Jul TIPS (when both exist), Jan→Jul order. A year with only
  one of the two (e.g. single-Feb 2040+) falls back to the one TIPS available.
- **`all`** — one TIPS per distinct maturity **month**, earliest→latest. Never both same-month
  issues — see coupon tie-break below. (How many months a year offers follows the issuance
  calendar, not a fixed schedule: see DATA_DICTIONARY §5.1 §Issuance-Dependent Values.)

**Same-month, two-issue tie-break (`couponPref`):** some months have two TIPS issues with different
coupons (Jan 2027/28/29, Apr 2028/29). `couponPref` (`'higher'` default, or `'lower'`) selects which
one wins whenever a policy must pick a single TIPS for that month — including `all`, which never
holds both. CUSIP is the final tiebreak (stable, independent of `tipsMap` iteration order) when
coupons tie exactly. **`last` never needs this**: the duplicate-issue months (Jan, Apr) are never
the latest month in their year, so the tie-break is unreachable under `last`.

**Build-only today.** The maturity and coupon controls, and picking maturities year by year, only
appear in Build; Rebalance always sizes with `maturityPref='last'` / `couponPref='higher'` and no
per-year picks. The rebalance *held* side already supports multiple TIPS per funded year (that
machinery predates this feature); only the rebalance *target* side is not yet multi-TIPS-aware (see
4.0 §ladder-core.js). Nothing in this section is specific to Build, so the rebalance target can use
it as written rather than defining its own version.

### Picking Maturities for a Funded Year

When none of the `maturityPref` choices gives the wanted result, the TIPS for a funded year may be
picked individually instead, via a fifth, **mutually exclusive** Maturity preference choice: **Select
maturities**. It is not a policy layered under `last`/`first`/`semiannual`/`all` — choosing it
replaces whichever policy was active, and opens a picker modal (one column per calendar month,
Low/High/All/None bulk actions per column) where individual TIPS are checked per year.

**Picking maturities is a dictate, not a preference.** A funded year with no explicit pick is not
filled by any `maturityPref` fallback — its DARA is forced to $0, so it is structurally present
(still resolved for bracket/gap classification) but funds nothing that year. This is the same
precedence a manual $0 per-year DARA entry already has. So Select maturities is an all-or-nothing
switch for the years it covers: every year in range either has an explicit pick or is intentionally
empty — there is no partial "policy-fill the years I didn't touch" behavior.

**The ladder's actual last year, while Select maturities is active, is the latest year with an
explicit pick** — the Last Year field is ignored for the build (and hidden, along with First Year,
since neither drives anything while Select is active). First Year still comes from its own field
value even though the field is hidden; only Last Year is overridden by the pick set.

Each selected maturity carries an **allocation weight** (see §Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split). Equal
weights — the default, and the only weights the selection UI writes today — reproduce the even split
exactly.

`couponPref` does not apply to a year whose TIPS were picked individually — when a month carries two
issues, the one that was picked is the one that is bought. It still governs every year filled by
`maturityPref` when Select maturities is not the active choice.

Maturities may be picked for a bracket year too. The bracket role stays with the latest-maturing
TIPS picked for that year (§Gap Years), so picking maturities can move the lower-side duration match.
Holding one maturity purely as bracket excess while a different maturity funds the year is
legitimate.

**Not yet persisted.** Per-year picks are prototype-only: they do not round-trip through the
CUSIP/Qty export or the `#params` block (see 2.1 §Broker Import) — reloading or importing a file
loses them, same as any other in-session-only UI state today.

### Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split

When a funded year holds N ≥ 1 TIPS (its `yearTipsListMap[year]` list, ordered by maturity), the
year's real P+I need is spread across them **by allocation weight**:

```
need    = yearDARA − runningLMI − excessLMI − future30yExtra    // same need as the N=1 case
share_i = need × w_i / Σw
for each TIPS i (earliest → latest), except the last:
  qty_i = MAX(0, ROUND(share_i / piPerBond_i))
last TIPS absorbs the rounding remainder:
  qty_last = MAX(0, ROUND((need − Σ delivered P+I of the other TIPS) / piPerBond_last))
```

**Equal weights are the default**, and reduce to `share = need / N` — the even split. Weights exist
so that uneven allocation within a year (more maturing in January than in October, say) is a change
of inputs rather than a second formula; there is one split rule, not one per case.

Each sub-rung is sized from its **own** P+I (`piPerBond_i` differs by coupon/maturity); the latest
maturity in the year absorbs the remainder so the year's total lands on `need`. Same-year sub-rungs
never feed each other's LMI — they mature in the same year, so a single year-level `need` is the
correct thing to split (not N independent per-TIPS DARAs). `N = 1` reproduces the legacy single-TIPS
sizing exactly, for any weight. The year's aggregate `fundedYearQty` and `annualInterest` (for LMI)
are the sum across sub-rungs.

**Spec reference:** 4.0 §`sizeYearRungs` / `aggregateRungs` (ladder-core.js).

## Gap Years

**[Gap Years](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#gap-years):** Years within the ladder period where TIPS have **not yet been issued** (currently 2037, 2038, 2039) — they will be issued in later years; until then the block is covered synthetically (build) or by bracket excess (held ladder).

**Synthetic TIPS for Gap Years:**
Created to maintain ladder continuity and facilitate duration matching.
- **Maturity**: January 15 of the gap year (10-year TIPS are issued in January and July).
- **Index ratio**: 1.0 (par assumption).
- **Price**: 100 (par).

**Gap Parameter Calculation:**
Synthetic TIPS are processed **longest to shortest** to model a hypothetical ladder as if gap TIPS existed. This establishes the **Gap Total Cost** (used for bracket sizing) and **Gap Avg Duration** (used for duration matching).

**Anchor when firstYear is inside the gap:** `anchorBefore` is always the **most recently issued** 10-year TIPS that is [ladder-eligible](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ladder-eligible-tips), i.e. the longest-dated 10-year maturity available below `minGapYear`. Jan 2036 as of 2026-07-27; Jul 2036 on issuance. When `firstYear = 2038` or `2039`, `minGapYear` is 2038 or 2039 (no 2037 or 2038 10-year TIPS exists), so the code walks backward from `minGapYear − 1` until it reaches that anchor.

**LMI for gap years** uses the same definition as for any other rung: annual coupon interest from ALL TIPS maturing after the gap year. This includes:
- **Actual TIPS LMI**: annual interest from funded years above the gap year. When `preLadderInterest` is active, only funded years with non-zero TIPS purchased contribute (zeroed funded years have no actual TIPS and therefore no coupon income).
- **Synthetic LMI** (`runningSynLMI`): accumulated synthetic interest from longer gap years already processed in this sweep (same accumulator pattern as the main ladder).

**Pre-Ladder Interest (PLI) — single interleaved pass** *(Build and Rebalance, when `preLadderInterest` enabled)*:
The PLI pool is distributed across ALL years in maturity order (short→long), with funded years and gap years interleaved. Gap years consume pool before longer-dated funded years (e.g., 2037–2039 are processed before 2040), so whether a funded year like 2040 gets zeroed depends on how much pool the gap years consumed first.
```
remaining = preLadderPool
for year in allYears ascending (short→long):   // allYears = rangeYears ∪ gapYears, sorted
  if year is a funded year:
    need = MAX(0, DARA - prelim[year].laterMatInt)
    if remaining >= need:
      zeroedFundedYears.add(year)
      remaining -= need
    else:
      partialCreditYear = year; partialCredit = remaining; remaining = 0; break
  if year is a gap year:
    need = MAX(0, DARA - actualTIPSLMI(year))   // full prelim LMI for all funded years above; synth LMI not yet known
    if remaining >= need:
      pliCredit[year] = need
      remaining -= need
    else:
      pliCredit[year] = remaining
      remaining = 0; break
```
After this pass, `zeroedFundedYears` is fully determined (including whether bracket years like 2040 are zeroed).

**Steps** (long→short qty sweep):
1. **Anchors**: `anchorBefore` (most recently issued ladder-eligible 10-year TIPS before the gap — the longest-dated 10-year maturity available; Jan 2036 as of 2026-07-27, Jul 2036 on issuance) and `anchorAfter` (earliest Feb TIPS after gap).
2. **Interpolate Yield**: Linear interpolation between anchors using maturity dates.
3. **Synthetic Coupon**: `MAX(0.00125, FLOOR(yield * 100 / 0.125) * 0.00125)`.
4. **Duration**: Calculate Modified Duration for each synthetic TIPS.
5. **PI per Synthetic TIPS**: `PI_y = 1,000 × (1 + coupon / 2)` (nPeriods = 1, Jan maturity; in dollars).
6. **Required Quantity**: `qty_y = MAX(0, ROUND((yearDARA_y − runningSynLMI − actualTIPSLMI − pliCredit[y]) / PI_y))` where `yearDARA_y = daraByYear.get(y) ?? DARA`.
   - `runningSynLMI` = accumulated synthetic interest from longer gap years already processed in this sweep.
   - `actualTIPSLMI` = sum of annual coupon interest from all actual funded years above this gap year.
   - `pliCredit[y]` = PLI pool credit allocated to this gap year (0 when `preLadderInterest` is disabled).
   - Synthetic TIPS in ladder 1 are constructed exactly like any other rung: LMI and PLI credit reduce the required quantity the same way they do for actual funded years.
7. **LMI Update**: `runningSynLMI += qty_y × 1,000 × coupon`. This accumulated synthetic interest **does** feed into the `runningSynLMI` term for shorter gap years in subsequent iterations.

## Gap Year Coverage Model

The gap coverage algorithm can be understood as two equivalent constructions:

**Component 1 — Synthetic ladder:**
Every year in the ladder period — funded years and gap years alike — is treated as a rung. Gap years receive synthetic TIPS (hypothetical, never purchased) sized so each gap year delivers ~DARA, exactly like any funded year. Each synthetic TIPS generates annual coupon that flows as LMI to shorter-maturity rungs, including the funded year immediately below the gap block. No excess exists anywhere in this view.

**Component 2 — Bracket excess:**
Same construction, but gap years have no TIPS. The theoretical cost of the synthetic gap TIPS is instead allocated to bracket excess holdings at the lower and upper bracket years, split by duration weight. The excess bonds' annual coupon replaces the LMI that the synthetic gap TIPS would have provided:
- **Upper bracket (2040) excess coupon** matures *after* the gap, so it is paid *through* the gap years (2037–2039). Like every other rung's coupon, it is income arriving in those years and **sizes the gap synthetic quantities down** — credited as LMI to all rungs shorter than 2040, the gap years included.
- **Lower bracket (2036) excess coupon** matures *before* the gap, so it flows only to the 2036 funded year and shorter rungs — never up into the gap years.

**Upper-bracket coupon feedback (fixpoint).** Because the upper-bracket excess *quantity* is sized from the gap's total cost, while that cost depends on the upper coupon credited into the gap, there is a one-step circular dependency. It is resolved by iterating to a fixed point (a shared routine used by both build and rebalance): size the gap → derive the upper excess → add its coupon to the gap's above-LMI → resize, until the upper excess quantity is stable (~2 iterations; the term is ~1% of gap cost). This makes the gap consistent with the rest of the ladder, where all coupon LMI (funded *and* excess) is credited. Omitting it would over-size the gap brackets by ~2 bonds each (~3% of gap cost) — the prior behavior.

A small residual remains: synthetic gap TIPS (par, coupon from yield interpolation) have different coupon rates than the actual bracket TIPS, and the fixpoint rounds the upper excess to whole bonds each step. This keeps the `avgAmt ≈ DARA` coverage invariant within ~0.6%.

**Bracket year ARA vs DARA:**
In the synthetic view, 2036 is a plain funded year sized for `ARA ≈ DARA`. In the actual ladder, the 2036 excess coupon also flows to the 2036 funded year as additional LMI, slightly reducing the funded-year quantity needed. The net effect is that the bracket year's total `ARA = DARA + small delta`, where the delta arises from the coupon rate difference between synthetic and actual TIPS. This is expected behavior, not a bug.

**LMI is always annual coupon interest only — never principal.** P+I (principal + last-year interest received at maturity) is a separate concept applied only to the funded year that holds the TIPS. Excess bracket bonds never contribute their principal to another rung's LMI.

**Excess coupon always flows inward:** Excess bracket coupon always contributes to LMI for shorter-maturity rungs within the ladder. The shortest rung in the ladder has no shorter rungs to receive the flow — the coupon is applied where shorter rungs exist, and there is no "exit" from the ladder.

## Future 30Y Rungs

**Future 30Y Rungs:** Years beyond the last-issued TIPS maturity year (currently 2056). Future 30Y rungs arise when `lastYear > 2056` (max `lastYear` = 2066).

**Hypothetical TIPS for Future 30Y Rungs:**
Processed **longest to shortest** using the longest-dated actual TIPS (currently 2056) as a flat-curve anchor:
- **Anchor yield**: Yield of the 2056 TIPS (flat-curve assumption).
- **Synthetic coupon**: `MAX(0.00125, FLOOR(yield × 100 / 0.125) × 0.00125)` — same formula as gap-year synthetic TIPS. Models the coupon a newly-issued 30Y TIPS would carry (nearest 0.125% below current yield); the 2056 TIPS actual coupon is not used.
- **Maturity**: February 15 of the Future 30Y year (30-year TIPS are issued in February).
- **Index ratio / Price**: 1.0 / 100 (par assumptions).
- **Duration**: Calculate Modified Duration using the synthetic coupon and anchor yield.
- **Quantity and Cost**: Calculated identically to Gap Years (see above) using the flat-curve anchor.

**Recovering the last funded year on import.** The Future 30Y rungs aren't held as TIPS — they are represented by the **cover-pair excess** (excess at the lower cover, 2056, and the upper cover, 2052), recorded in the import's excess column. So the intended last funded year is recovered by **reverse-engineering the Future 30Y duration match**: the cost-weighted split between upper- and lower-cover excess is a pure duration relationship — independent of DARA and increasing with the number of rungs — so the last funded year is the one whose cover split, computed exactly as the build computes it, reproduces the imported split. This is reliable because the excess column is a calculated field populated only by this app's export; the recovered year is user-overridable for the off-nominal case of a hand-edited file. When there is no cover excess, the ladder simply ends at the last issued TIPS. (The gap block recovers its *first* funded year from the same idea; that side can use a simpler excess-to-DARA ratio, but the ratio shortcut does **not** transfer to Future 30Y — its upper cover is deep-discount and a long block carries a large later-maturity-interest cascade — so the duration-match reversal is used here instead.)

## Future 30Y Cover AMD (Accrued Market Discount)

**Both Future 30Y covers (the 2052 upper and the 2056 lower) carry a market discount** because their coupons (0.125%) are far below current yields (~2.7%), so both are modeled for AMD. The worked example below uses the 2052 (the deeper discount); the 2056 schedule is identical with its own bond/exQty/maturity. **AMD is treated exactly like coupon interest from these low-coupon TIPS** (this is **Option C** — even, held-to-maturity). A par bond returns its whole yield as coupon; a zero-coupon bond returns it all as accreted discount; the 2052 sits near the zero-coupon end — almost all of its yield arrives as price accretion toward par, not as the tiny coupon. Under the constant-yield method that accretion **is** interest above the coupon.

**The income is what the full held excess position earns each year — modeled held-to-maturity (settlement → the 2052's own maturity), independent of how many bonds are sold.** Selling some excess 2052s is only the mechanism that turns the accrued discount into spendable cash (each year, bonds are sold to realize the market discount that accrued over the prior year); it is **not** what defines the income, exactly as a coupon is earned on the whole position regardless of which bonds you later sell. The number sold and the income are therefore **decoupled** — the schedule does not deplete the income basis. This AMD cash flow reduces the number of funded-year bonds needed, analogous to LMI from coupon interest.

**Applies to excess holdings only.** Funded-year 2052 bonds are held to maturity and receive full par — their discount is already captured in P+I. Applying AMD to them would double-count it.

### Income each year — interest on the full held position

For each year, the per-bond real price is taken under the constant-yield method, kept in today's real dollars by applying the **settlement** index ratio at every point (nothing projects future inflation, consistent with rate, coupon LMI, and maturity principal elsewhere):
```
date(Y)       = last calendar day of February of year Y       (≈ 30Y issuance / secondary-purchase date)
adjPrice(Y)   = priceFromYield(yield_2052, coupon_2052, date(Y), maturity_2052) / 100 × indexRatio_settle × 1000
adjPrice(settlement) = costPerBond_2052                       // cost basis
adjPrice(Y ≥ maturity_2052) = parPerBond = indexRatio_settle × 1000   // at/after maturity → par
a(Y)          = adjPrice(Y) − adjPrice(Y−1)                   // this year's accretion increment, per bond
```
`a(Y)` is the per-bond interest accrued during year Y — the basis steps up each year to `adjPrice(Y)`, so the next year counts only the next increment (no double-counting).

### AMD income by funded year

```
future30yUpperAnnualAmd(Y) = future30yUpperExQty × a(Y)     for Y in (settlementYear, maturityYear_2052]
                           = 0                                otherwise
```
The income is on the **full, undepleted** excess — `future30yUpperExQty` is constant across years, **not** reduced by any roll. The range runs every year from `settlementYear + 1` through the 2052's maturity year (`settlementYear` itself contributes 0 — no accretion booked at the cost basis). Every funded year in `[firstYear, lastYear]` within that span therefore receives an AMD credit, not just an early sale window.

**Profile: even, gently back-loaded by convexity.** A deep-discount bond accretes slightly faster toward par as it approaches maturity, so `a(Y)` rises modestly over time; the annual AMD is roughly level with a gentle upward tilt. **Conserving:** `Σ a(Y) = parPerBond − costPerBond_2052`, so the lifetime AMD per bond is exactly the total market discount — no residual, no rescaling.

**Use in fyQty formula:** AMD is subtracted from DARA alongside `runningLMI` — it is NOT added to `runningLMI`:
```
fyQty(Y) = MAX(0, ROUND((dara − runningLMI − future30yUpperAnnualAmd(Y) − future30yRollCoupon(Y)) / piPerBond))
```

**Generic over discount excess holdings.** The schedule is computed by `excessAmdSchedule({ bond, exQty, ... })` — nothing in it is 2052-specific. **Both Future-30Y covers (2052 and 2056) are wired into `sizeLadder`'s `amdExcessBonds` list**, each crediting AMD over its own life (the 2056's runs settlement → 2056). The 2036/2040 gap brackets carry some discount too and can be added to the same list for full accountability; sizing, the cover Amount net-out, and rebalance all pick them up automatically once added. The per-funded-year credit is the **combined** AMD across all wired covers; the cover Amount net-out is keyed by each cover's own bracket year (`amdLifetimeByBracketYear`).

### Cover-roll coupon (post-upper-maturity funded years 2053–2056)

AMD runs settlement → the 2052's maturity (2052). After the 2052 matures (at par), the dollars it stood for (its cost basis) are reinvested into the actual Future-30Y TIPS, which then pay coupon at the Future-30Y rate. For funded years strictly **between the upper cover's maturity and the first Future-30Y year (2053–2056)**, the upper-cover share of that coupon is real income that sizes those years down — a seamless hand-off from the 2052 AMD (the "rough equivalence" of the excess TIPS' interest+AMD and the Future-30Y coupon that reinvestment buys).
```
future30yRollCoupon(Y) = future30yUpperWeight × future30ySeedLMI    for upperMatYear < Y < min(future30yYears)
                       = 0                                          otherwise
```
where `future30ySeedLMI` is the synthetic block's total annual coupon and `future30yUpperWeight` is the upper cover's duration-match share. (Held-based equivalent used in the rebalance ARA recovery: `heldUpperExcess × costPerBond_2052 × syntheticCoupon(yield_2056)`.)

- **Non-cascading**, exactly like AMD: credited only to 2053–2056, **not** added to `runningLMI`. Years ≤ 2052 are already credited via AMD (the 2052 is still held then), so cascading the roll coupon below 2053 would double-count.
- The **lower cover (2056)** needs no analog — no funded year sits between it and the block, and its own coupon already flows down as ordinary excess LMI.
- Threaded identically to AMD: subtracted in `fyQty`/`fundedYearAmount`, shown as its own line item ("Future-30Y coupon (2052 roll)"), and (for ladders starting after 2053) summed into the pre-ladder pool as `preLadderRollCouponPool`.

**Pre-Ladder Interest:** AMD for years before `firstYear` (i.e. `(settlementYear, firstYear)`) is summed into the PLI pool exactly like coupon interest; the PLI zeroing algorithm credits it to the earliest funded years. `settlementYear` itself contributes 0 (no accretion yet). The pool has **two named sources**, surfaced separately so the credit is fully traceable: `preLadderCouponPool` (`preLadderYears × annual ladder coupon income`) and `preLadderAmdPool` (`Σ AMD(Y)` for pre-ladder years); `preLadderPool = preLadderCouponPool + preLadderAmdPool`.

**Rebalance before-state:** the held quantity of each cover differs from its rebuilt target excess, so the Before schedule is rebuilt **per cover from the actual held excess** (`excessAmdSchedule({ bond, exQty: heldExcess })`) and summed — not a single linear rescale, since the held proportions of 2052 vs 2056 need not match the target split. The cover Amount net-out scales each cover's lifetime AMD by `exQty / (that cover's own target excess)`.

**Display:** `future30yUpperAnnualAmd` is shown as a separate line item ("AMD from excess TIPS") in the Amount popup for each funded year where the in-ladder AMD is non-zero. It does not appear in the LMI line. The **pre-ladder** AMD is not double-shown there; instead the "Pre-ladder credit" line drills (Level-3) into the pool composition, which breaks out **"Pre-ladder AMD from excess TIPS"** as its own line alongside "Pre-ladder coupon interest" — so the AMD that was realized in the pre-ladder years remains visible and traceable, consistent with the ladder's transparency goal.

*The 2056 lower cover is modeled identically (Rev 7). Its AMD runs settlement → 2056, so funded years 2053–2056 carry both the 2052 cover-roll coupon and the 2056-cover AMD (distinct income: rolled-2052 coupon vs still-held-2056 accretion — no double-count). Remaining future extension: the near-par 2036/2040 gap brackets could be added to `amdExcessBonds` for a uniform ledger; the effect is small.*

## Duration Matching (Brackets)

Missing blocks (Gap or Future 30Y) are covered by additional holdings in a **bracket pair** (or **cover pair**). The goal is for the excess holdings to change in value identically to the missing block if rates move.

### Average Block Duration is Cost-Weighted
The representative duration of a missing block (`averageBlockDuration` / `avgGapDuration` / future-30Y `avgDuration`) is the **cost-weighted** mean of its constituent synthetic/hypothetical rung durations — applied to **both** the gap block and the future-30Y block:
```
averageBlockDuration = Σ(rungCost × rungDur) / Σ rungCost,    rungCost = rung.qty × 1000
```
Cost-weighting is required because per-year DARA may be uneven: a more heavily-funded rung carries more cost and must pull the average toward its own duration. (A simple mean would mis-weight uneven funding.) When `Σ rungCost = 0`, fall back to the simple mean of rung durations.

### 2-Bracket Weights (Standard)
```
lowerWeight * d_lower + upperWeight * d_upper = averageBlockDuration
upperWeight = (averageBlockDuration - d_lower) / (d_upper - d_lower)
lowerWeight = 1 - upperWeight
```

**Lower bracket always exists (when gap years are in range):** When `firstYear < 2040` and gap years are present, the lower bracket — the [Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket): the **latest-maturing** ladder-eligible TIPS with maturity strictly below `minGapYear`, found by scanning `tipsMap` — always exists. Jan 2036 as of 2026-07-27 (a Jul 2036 has been auctioned but is not yet issued, and becomes the active lower bracket on issuance). **Latest-maturing, not "the latest January":** a pre-gap year may hold both a Jan and a Jul maturity, and the later one gives the better duration match, so it is the one used. Earlier maturities in that same year, if held, are funded-year holdings — they carry no bracket role. When `firstYear` is inside the gap (2037–2039), the lower bracket year (Jan 2036) is below `firstYear`; it is inserted into `yearBondMap`/`rangeYears` before the sweep with `fundedYearQty = 0` (pure bracket excess, no funded year component). Duration-matching always uses the standard two-sided formula with this lower bracket. When `firstYear ≥ 2040`, no gap years exist in the ladder range and bracket logic does not apply. **Rebalance**: the active lower bracket is always this canonical maturity, regardless of existing holdings. With **Retain lower bracket excess** off, any older lower bracket maturity (e.g. Jan 2034) falls into the normal rebalance sweep and is rebuilt to funded-year qty only; with it on, that excess is retained per §Retained Bracket Excess.

### Retained Bracket Excess (Rebalance)

Applies when holdings contain lower-side excess in maturities **older** than the [Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket) — [Retained Bracket Excess](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#retained-bracket-excess). This arises naturally over time: each rebalance buys the then-active lower bracket, and when a later maturity is issued the previous one becomes retained. Jan 2034 alongside Jan 2036 is one such case; Jan 2034 *and* Jan 2036 alongside Jul 2036 is the next.

**The structure is not named by a count.** Any number of retained maturities may accumulate, so the arrangement is never named by how many brackets it holds — the rule below is the same for zero retained generations or five. `bracketWeightsN`, the solver below, already generalizes to any number of retained legs; only the code that identifies *which* holdings are retained currently stops at one generation (see note at the end of this section).

Retained excess is not priced as though it sat at the active lower bracket's duration — it is older and therefore shorter, so doing that leaves the block under-matched (the realized blend lands short of `dGap`). Instead each retained maturity is **frozen at its own duration** and only the active lower bracket and the upper bracket are solved for:

```
R   = Σ (retainedExcessCost_r) / totalBlockCost           (retained share of the block, frozen)
D_R = Σ (retainedExcessCost_r / totalBlockCost) × d_r     (retained legs' duration contribution)

upperWeight       = ((dGap − D_R) − (1 − R) × d_activeLower) / (d_upper − d_activeLower)
activeLowerWeight = (1 − R) − upperWeight
```

This is `bracketWeightsN` (4.0 §Computation Modules, `gap-math.js`) — the retained legs enter the duration-match equation `Σ wᵣ·dᵣ + w_active·d_active + w_upper·d_upper = dGap` as fixed inputs; only `w_active` and `w_upper` are unknowns.

**Over-allocated** (no non-negative `(activeLowerWeight, upperWeight)` solves the equation above):
- Sell retained excess **oldest maturity first**, taking each to zero before touching the next, re-solving after each step, until a feasible solution exists.
- Sell the active lower bracket only if still infeasible after every retained maturity is depleted (falls back to the plain two-sided `bracketWeights(d_activeLower, d_upper, dGap)`).

**Under-allocated** (feasible with all retained excess frozen at its current quantity):
- Freeze all retained excess at its current quantity — **never increased**.
- Buy the active lower bracket to cover the remainder.

For each lower-side maturity `b`:
`excessQtyAfter_b = ROUND(targetCost_b / costPerBond_b)`

Only one retained maturity (the orig lower bracket, one generation older than the active lower bracket) is currently identified from holdings and passed to `bracketWeightsN`; identifying multiple retained generations from holdings is a known gap, tracked separately. The active lower bracket is always resolved from `tipsMap` by the rule, never from holdings.

**User control — "Retain lower bracket excess"** (Rebalance only; on by default). When **off**, retained excess is sold outright and the bracket role consolidates into the active lower bracket; those maturities fall into the normal rebalance sweep and are rebuilt to funded-year quantity only. When **on**, the priority rule above applies. The toggle governs the **excess** only — funded-year holdings in those maturities are unaffected either way.

### Future 30Y Cover Pair
When `lastYear > 2056`, two existing TIPS are used to duration-match the hypothetical Future 30Y rungs:
- **Future 30Y lower cover**: Always the 2056 TIPS.
- **Future 30Y upper cover**: The TIPS with the longest modified duration excluding 2056 (currently 2052).

#### LMI at the 2056/Future 30Y Boundary

The 2056 funded year's LMI will appear low — and lower than the LMI shown for the 2057 hypothetical rung — for two structural reasons that are correct, not bugs:

1. **No actual bonds exist above 2056.** Future 30Y rungs (2057–2066) are hypothetical; their coupon income is synthetic, used only to size hypothetical quantities. None of it flows into the actual ladder's `runningLMI` pool.

2. **Duration constraint forces weight into 2052.** The hypothetical future30y TIPS are long-dated (~30–40 year modified duration from settlement). The 2056 TIPS has a higher coupon and therefore shorter duration than needed to match the average. `bracketWeights` must assign substantial weight to the 2052 TIPS (near-zero coupon, acts as a near-zero-coupon bond, long duration) to reach the target. The 2052 excess bonds mature before 2056 and their coupons flow only to years ≤ 2051 — they contribute nothing to 2056's LMI. Only the 2056 excess bonds (the lower-weight fraction) contribute coupon income to 2056.

The apparent inversion — `LMI(2057 hypothetical) > LMI(2056 actual)` — is therefore an apples-vs-oranges comparison: the 2057 figure is synthetic LMI from within `calcFuture30yParams`, while the 2056 figure is actual income from purchased bonds. The 2056 funded year is correctly sized as long as `ARA(2056) ≈ DARA`.

### Excess Quantities
For each bracket `b`:
```
excessQty_b = ROUND(totalBlockCost × weight_b / costPerBond_b)
```
where `costPerBond_b = price/100 × indexRatio × 1000` (per **TIPS Basics** — IR always included). This formula always applies to the upper bracket (2040), and to the lower side whenever no retained bracket excess is present.

**Exception — retained bracket excess:** when lower-side excess exists in maturities older than the active lower bracket, the priority rule (see §Retained Bracket Excess) replaces the weight formula across the lower side. The upper bracket (2040) still uses the standard formula above.

### Excess Amount (Bracket / Cover Display)
The **Amount** shown for a bracket/cover row is the **coverage the excess provides to the missing block** — it should total ≈ `(number of missing years) × DARA`, exactly like a funded year's Amount is the real amount delivered that year. (Gap: ≈ `numGapYears × DARA`; Future-30Y cover pair: ≈ `numFuture30yYears × DARA`.) The raw P+I of the excess bonds alone understates this, because the synthetic block used LMI from longer-dated rungs to size its quantities down; that LMI is added back. A deep-discount cover (both the 2052 and the 2056) needs one further correction: its accretion is **delivered to earlier funded years as AMD**, not to this block, so that lifetime AMD is **netted out** to avoid double-counting it.
```
blockLMITotal_b  = the income that sized the block's synthetic rungs down, by block:
                     gap     → gapLMITotal      = Σ over gap years (laterMatInt_y + pliCredit_y + amd_y)
                     future30y→ future30yLMITotal = Σ over future-30y rungs (laterMatInt_y)   // intra-block coupon
excessAmt_b      = excessQty_b × piPerBond_b − amdLifetime_b + weight_b × blockLMITotal_b
```
where `piPerBond_b` is the bond's P+I per bond (incl. index ratio) and `amdLifetime_b` is the total AMD that bond's excess delivers to earlier years (Σ of its AMD schedule; see §Future 30Y Cover AMD). **Both Future-30Y covers (2052 and 2056) have `amdLifetime_b > 0`**; the near-par gap brackets (2036/2040) are still 0 (not yet modeled). The net-out is keyed by bracket year and scaled by `exQty / (that cover's own target excess)`, so once 2036/2040 gain AMD schedules their Amount auto-corrects. Note the **lower cover's Amount can sit above its raw par P+I**: its large LMI add-back (high duration-match weight) exceeds its AMD — correct, not a bug.

Example (fixture, 40K DARA, 2057–2066): with only the 2052 netted the total read `405,970`; netting the 2056 too gives `2052 228,641 + 2056 171,372 = 400,013 ≈ 400,000 = 10 × DARA`. The removed amounts (175,366 on 2052, ≈83,886 on 2056) are exactly each cover's AMD already credited to its earlier funded years.

## Algorithm (Longest-to-Shortest Sweep)

Process actual bonds and funded years **longest to shortest**.

1. **Initialize LMI Pool**: `runningLMI = 0`.
2. **For each year Y (longest to shortest)**:
   a. **Incoming LMI**: Sum of inflation-adjusted `annualInterest` from all bonds maturing *after* Y.
   b. **Bracket Excess Contribution**: If year Y contains bracket excess bonds, their coupon interest contributes to the pool for year Y itself.
      - `excessLMI_Y = excessQty_Y × annualInterestPerBond_Y`
   c. **Target FY Quantity**:
      - `Required_PI = YearDARA - (IncomingLMI + excessLMI_Y)`
      - `fundedYearQty_Y = MAX(0, ROUND(Required_PI / piPerBond_Y))`
   d. **Update LMI Pool**:
      - `runningLMI += (fundedYearQty_Y + excessQty_Y) × annualInterestPerBond_Y`
3. **Gap Years**: Contribute zero to the `runningLMI` pool (no actual bonds held; their role is played by bracket excess in other years).

## Transparency & UI Requirements

Duration matching must be educational and traceable. The UI must provide:
1. **Individual Disclosure**: Every synthetic/hypothetical bond used to calculate the average must be listed with its own Modified Duration.
2. **Derivation**: The "Average Duration" must be explicitly shown as the cost-weighted mean of the listed bonds (weighted by each rung's cost = qty × 1000).
3. **Fulcrum Visualization**: A visual representation showing the bracket durations flanking the average duration.
4. **Cost-to-Quantity Chain**: A breakdown showing how the block's total cost is weighted and then converted to discrete bond units.
