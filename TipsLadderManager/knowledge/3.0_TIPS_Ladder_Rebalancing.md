## Purpose

Rebalance an existing TIPS ladder to align it with its desired-amount targets (DARA), **which may vary year to year** (see 2.0 §Duration Matching — per-year DARA may be uneven). There is a **single rebalance**: it rebuilds the ladder from the current portfolio toward the per-year DARA targets, buying and selling TIPS as required. (An earlier "Gap" mode that did minimal trades was scrapped; the `method` parameter, the `#method` UI input, and the `isFullMode`/`!isFullMode` branches have been removed. `runRebalance` no longer takes a `method`.)

## Context

TIPS ladders require periodic rebalancing as new TIPS are issued — filling former gap years so their coverage can move from bracket excess into the year's own rung — and as market prices drift the income profile. A rebalance realigns the ladder to its per-year DARA targets using the current portfolio.

## Rebalancing-Specific Concepts

### Official Ladder Range

- **firstYear**: Earliest year with holdings
- **lastYear**: Latest contiguous year with holdings before any missing rung above 2040.
- Missing rungs above 2040 truncate the official range (e.g., holdings in 2047 and 2049 but not 2048 → lastYear = 2047)
- **Structural gap years** (currently 2037-2039) are the consecutive block of years immediately before 2040 where no TIPS have been issued. Derived by walking backward from 2039 until a year with TIPS is found in market data. Years outside this structural block (2036 and below) that have no current holdings are treated as funded-year rungs needing addition, not gap years. Structural gap years do NOT break contiguity if 2040 is held — **mechanism**: if 2040 is held, the range unconditionally extends through the gap years.
- **Non-contiguous TIPS > 2040**: Contribute annual interest to earlier years but are NOT counted as rungs for DARA calculation or ladder range determination unless `lastYearOverride` extends the range to include them.
- **`lastYearOverride`** (optional UI parameter): When provided, replaces the derived `lastYear`. Allows the user to declare the intended last funded year, which may be a gap year (e.g., 2039) or an extended year (e.g., 2042). The UI infers a sensible default and shows a dropdown for manual adjustment.
  - **Shortening**: When `lastYearOverride < derivedLastYear`, all holdings in years `(lastYearOverride, derivedLastYear]` that were contiguous with the original ladder are sold in full (produce sell rows in output). These years are excluded from the rebuild.
  - **Extending**: When `lastYearOverride > derivedLastYear`, years in `(derivedLastYear, lastYearOverride]` with no current holdings are treated as rebalance years — the appropriate TIPS for those years are identified from market data and buy rows are generated.
- **`firstYear` UI inference**: When a Format 4/5 CSV is loaded, if `derivedFirstYear` is a pre-gap Jan TIPS year (currently 2036) and all its holdings have `excessQty = qty` (pure bracket, no funded component), the UI infers the true `firstYear` using: `numGapYears = round(totalExcessPI / roughDARA)` → `firstYear = 2040 − numGapYears`. `totalExcessPI = excessQty(2036) × piPerBond(2036) + excessQty(2040) × piPerBond(2040)`. `roughDARA` = median ARA of non-bracket, non-gap funded years. This works because bracket excess P+I ≈ `numGapYears × DARA` (spec §Gap Year Coverage Model). The inferred gap year becomes the default dropdown selection. For Format 1/2/3 CSVs (no `excessQty`), no inference is attempted and `firstYear` defaults to `derivedFirstYear`.
- **`firstYearOverride`** (optional UI parameter): When provided, replaces the derived `firstYear` (earliest year with holdings). Holdings in years `[derivedFirstYear, firstYearOverride)` are sold in full (produce sell rows in output). These years are excluded from the rebuild. **The per-year DARA table follows the same override**: it is keyed off `rebal-first-year` when set, not always off the earliest held year, and any stored per-year value outside the resulting `[firstYear, lastYear]` is dropped from the table — narrowing First Year removes the excluded year's row rather than leaving a stale one behind.
  - **firstYear as a gap year**: When `firstYearOverride ∈ {2037, 2038, 2039}` (gap years), the ladder starts inside the structural gap block. No holdings exist below the gap, but the active lower bracket (most recently issued 10-year TIPS below the gap; Jan 2036 as of 2026-07-27) is still identified from `tipsMap`. `identifyBrackets` falls back to `tipsMap` when the holdings search yields no lower bracket, setting `lowerCUSIP`, `lowerYear`, and `lowerMaturity` from `tipsMap`. Standard two-sided duration-matching weights apply. In full rebalance, a BUY order for 2036 excess is generated (`qtyBefore = 0`, `excessQtyAfter = ROUND(totalBlockCost × lowerWeight / costPerBond_2036)`). Build: the 2036 bond is inserted into `yearBondMap`/`rangeYears` before the sweep with `fundedYearQty = 0` (pure bracket excess). The bracket identification step excludes holdings from years < `firstYear` (being sold) so they don't incorrectly satisfy the lower-bracket search.
  - **Retained excess with firstYear inside the gap**: When retention is on and `firstYear ∈ {2037, 2038, 2039}`, `minGapYear = firstYear` and `minGapYear − 1` is itself a gap year with no 10-year TIPS. The active lower bracket search must walk `tipsMap` for the longest-dated 10-year maturity strictly below `minGapYear` (same logic as `anchorBefore`) rather than checking year `minGapYear − 1` exactly. This yields Jan 2036, which is also the `brackets.lowerYear` from `identifyBrackets`. With no retained maturity distinct from it, the lower side reduces to the plain two-sided case — correct behavior, no error.

### lastYear as a Gap Year

When `lastYearOverride` is a gap year (i.e., `lastYear ∈ gapYears`), the upper bracket year (2040) is **not a funded rung** — all its holdings are treated as bracket excess:

- `fundedYearQty(2040) = 0` in Phase 3 and Phase 4 (`yearDara(2040) = 0`)
- `excessQtyBefore(2040)` follows the standard cost formula (`ROUND(totalBlockCost × weight_2040 / costPerBond_2040)`). Since `fyQtyAfter(2040) = 0`, `fyQtyBefore = qtyBefore − excessQtyBefore` approaches zero when the portfolio was correctly built.
- Phase 2 (`calculateGapParameters`): 2040's LMI contribution uses **actual current holdings qty** (not a target computed from DARA) since no funded-year target exists for 2040.
- `rungCount` = `lastYear − firstYear + 1` (gap years count as rungs; their income is funded by bracket excess)

**UI inference for default lastYear**: After loading holdings, if the last maturity is 2040, `lastYear` defaults to 2040 (or the last gap year, 2039, if the gap ends immediately before). If the last maturity is before 2040, that year is used. The dropdown includes all available TIPS years to allow extension.

### RefCPI Date Override

By default, RefCPI is looked up for the settlement date. The UI allows overriding the reference date (click the RefCPI value in the info strip). When overridden:
- All P+I, cost, and ARA calculations use the RefCPI for the selected date instead of the settlement date
- The per-year DARA mirror (computed from holdings at load) is cleared — rerun to refresh
- The info strip shows the override date in grey next to the RefCPI value

**Date picker input**: the shared native date picker (`shared/src/date-picker.js`, class `.date-picker`) — the browser's native calendar/keyboard entry, value in ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`. It sits inside the override popup; pressing Enter or clicking Apply commits the date; Reset reverts to the default settlement date.

**Ref CPI interpolation popup** (level-3 drill from any Ref CPI row): shows the active ref CPI date (not today's date) as "Settlement Date", and displays the m-3 and m-2 CPI-U values that correspond to that date. The date passed to `buildRefCpiDrill` is always `activeRefCpiDateStr ?? _defaultRefCpiDateStr`, formatted mm/dd/yyyy.

### DARA

#### Per-Year DARA from Portfolio (File Load) — mirror current holdings, **no inference**

When a holdings file **without an explicit `#fundedYear,dara` block** (broker, legacy Format-5, tipsladder Format-4) is loaded in Rebalance mode, the per-year DARA panel is populated to **mirror the current portfolio** — the Annual Real Amount (ARA) each rung already throws off. **No DARA is inferred, scaled, or self-finance-solved at load.** (Our own exports carry the `#fundedYear,dara` block and take the exact-reproduction path instead — see 2.1 Broker Import.)

1. **Raw ARA, every in-range year**: process holdings longest-to-shortest, accumulating LMI (annual coupon from later bonds). For a **held** year `ARA_year = Σ(qty × piPerBond) + LMI_from_later`; for an ordinary **empty** in-range year (TIPS exist for that maturity, the user simply holds none) `ARA_year = LMI_from_later` alone. Every year in `[firstYear, lastYear]` is written to the per-year store — empty years included — so the panel shows each rung's true current income. An empty year between two holdings shows just the coupon dripping down from the later bond (e.g. ~$230), **not** a full rung.
2. **Gap years use the funded-year median**: a structural **gap year** (2037–2039 — no TIPS issued for anyone, identified by `getGapYears(tipsMap)`) cannot be held directly, so there is no holding to read a DARA from. The bare LMI dripping down would understate the rung (it is funded by bracket excess at the adjacent years, not by a bond of its own). Each gap year with no supplied DARA therefore falls back to the **funded-year median** (step 3). *(Future-30Y years 2057+ are out of scope for basic files — they only appear via our own export, which carries an explicit `#fundedYear,dara` block.)*
3. **Bracket-candidate excess flag (held years only)**: the median is taken over the **held** funded years; empty LMI-only years are excluded so they cannot collapse the median. A gap-adjacent bracket-candidate year whose raw ARA exceeds that median is no longer silently capped to it (the old 1.5×-median cap is removed). Instead the year is **flagged** and its per-year store entry is left unset — see §Before-State Preview and Bracket-Year Excess Detection below for the full mechanism (which candidate years are checked, how a single lower-bracket candidate is chosen, and what happens once the user supplies its DARA). This is a **preview-time** flag only; it does not change how any other year in this mirror is populated.
4. **Scalar DARA field**: set to the held-year median (rounded) — the representative rung size, used as the fallback only when the user later clears the per-year store and types a single scalar.
5. **Panel auto-opens** so the user can review or modify individual years before running.

This per-year store is the **shape** the rebalance preserves. At **load** nothing is scaled or solved — the panel shows the portfolio as-is, **except** the flagged bracket-candidate year(s) from step 3, whose store entry is deliberately left unset pending user input (§Before-State Preview and Bracket-Year Excess Detection) — every other year mirrors unconditionally. The self-financing adjustment happens at **Run** (next subsection). The user may also **explicitly** reshape before running: type a new scalar in the DARA field, edit individual years, or select a range of rungs directly on the table and use Set DARA / Infer DARA (see §Per-Rung DARA Selection). Nothing else is inferred on the user's behalf at load.

#### Before-State Preview and Bracket-Year Excess Detection

**The Before state renders immediately on load, not gated behind Run.** As soon as holdings are loaded / account(s) selected in Rebalance mode, the table renders Qty Before / Cost Before / Amount Before for every ordinary funded year, computed directly from current holdings + market data (price, index ratio) — no DARA, no duration matching. This is a **standalone computation**, architecturally decoupled from `runRebalance`/`runFundedRebalance` (§Funding the rebalance below): it does not call, and must not import, the duration-match/self-financing engine. This is not a second copy of that math (single-source-of-truth, top-level CLAUDE.md) — it is a genuinely different, simpler computation (holdings valuation) with no engine involved, not a duplicate of the sizing algorithm. After-side columns (Qty/Cost/Amount After, Qty Δ, Cash Δ — 5.0 §Rebalance-only columns) and the After-dependent controls (Export CSV, Export CUSIP/Qty, Trade Ticket, Duration links, Avg Yld/Avg Mod Dur — 6.0 §Form Card Layout) stay blank/hidden exactly as before, until the user clicks **Rebalance Ladder**. Run itself is unchanged: it still runs the full duration-match/self-financing engine exactly as today and fills in the After side. **Expand/Collapse All** (6.0 §Collapsible Groups) is NOT in that hidden set — the ladder table with its collapsible funded-year groups is already fully visible pre-Run, so those two buttons are available as soon as this before-state table renders.

**Bracket-year excess detection** runs at the moment of this preview, before any Run, over candidate years that are structurally fixed (not duration-matched):
- **Lower bracket candidates**: 2032–2035 (`LOWEST_LOWER_BRACKET_YEAR` through the year below `minGapYear` — DATA_DICTIONARY §5.1, §Bracket Identification Rules above).
- **Active Lower Bracket candidate**: the [Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket) year itself (2036 today).
- **Upper bracket candidate**: 2040.
- **Future 30Y cover candidates**: 2052 and 2056 (2.0 §Future 30Y Cover Pair).

For each candidate year that is currently **held**, its raw ARA (step 1 above — held qty's P+I plus incoming LMI) is compared to the **held-year median** (step 3 above — held funded years only, excluding the candidate itself):
- Raw ARA ≤ median: **no flag** — the year renders as an ordinary funded year, no special treatment, no Gap sub-row.
- Raw ARA > median, **lower bracket (2032–2035 — every Jan-maturity TIPS below the Active Lower Bracket year; the active bracket itself is evaluated separately, below, not part of this pool)**: if exactly one candidate exceeds the median, that year is flagged. If more than one exceeds, only the one with the **highest excess** (`rawARA − median`) is flagged — the same "Excess ARA Priority" rule §Bracket Identification Rules §Retained Maturities already uses for the real engine's retained-maturity identification (highest `araByYear[y] − DARA` wins, not latest-maturing) — and the others render as ordinary funded years. (An earlier revision of this section said "latest-maturing wins"; that picked the wrong year on real holdings whenever the largest retained excess sits at an earlier-maturing candidate than a smaller-excess later one — fixed to match the engine's own established rule instead of inventing a different one for the preview.)
- Raw ARA > median, **Active Lower Bracket (2036)**, **upper bracket (2040)**, and **Future 30Y covers (2052, 2056)**: each is evaluated independently against the same held-year median (if held) — none of these compete against each other or against the lower-bracket pool above for a single slot; any subset may flag simultaneously. The active bracket is deliberately kept out of the lower-bracket pool's "highest excess wins" competition — it's expected to carry both real funded income and its own gap-coverage excess, so it must be able to flag alongside a genuine older retained-leg flag (e.g. 2034) rather than always winning that pool's tie-break by virtue of usually holding the largest raw ARA.

**When a year is flagged**, the app does not silently cap it and hide the excess (replacing the old step-3 cap above) — but it also doesn't leave the year blank pending user input. Instead it shows an immediate **best guess**: the flagged year's DARA input (5.0 §Funded Year Group Header Row §DARA Input) is pre-filled with the held-year median (the same median used for detection), and the excess — `raw ARA − median` — is shown right away in that year's Gap sub-row, exactly as if it had been entered normally. The pre-filled value is marked with the existing amber ⚠ "auto-set to median" indicator (already used elsewhere in this app for auto-capped years — `_bracketAutoCapYears`/`_daraByYearRow`'s `bracketFlag` in `index.html`; same tooltip language: "Auto-set to median: holdings suggest bracket excess. Adjust if this year is intended for higher income."), signalling it's a guess pending review, not a confirmed figure.
- A flagged lower-bracket, Active Lower Bracket, or 2040 candidate's excess is presented as covering the gap years it apparently funds (2037–2039, 2.0 §Gap Years) — the gap years' own DARA inputs are also pre-filled/flagged the same way where they have no other source.
- A flagged 2052/2056 candidate's excess is presented as covering the Future 30Y years it apparently funds (the Future 30Y years in `[firstYear, lastYear]`, 2.0 §Future 30Y Rungs), same treatment.

If the user edits a flagged year's DARA input to a different value, the ⚠ clears for that year and the excess recalculates immediately — `raw ARA − entered DARA` — updating the Gap sub-row live. This is plain subtraction, never duration-matching, for both the guessed and the user-confirmed figure.

**Qty Before / Cost Before split for a flagged year.** For every OTHER (unflagged) year these never depend on the funded/excess split — the full held quantity is simply the funded-side figure, as before. But for a flagged year, showing the full held qty/cost against a much smaller (guessed or entered) Amount reads as broken (a funded row with a large qty/cost next to a tiny Amount, and an excess sub-row with a dollar figure but no qty/cost backing it). Once the year's DARA is known (guessed or entered), split qty/cost too, using the same LMI-based conversion this app already uses elsewhere for DARA→qty (§Intentional empty rungs above: `round((DARA − LMI) / piPerBond)`) — still not duration-matching, just direct bond arithmetic:
- `fundedYearQtyBefore = round((DARA − LMI_before) / piPerBond)` (`LMI_before` is the year's own incoming later-maturity interest, already computed for the raw-ARA figure above; `piPerBond` is the flagged CUSIP's own P+I per bond)
- `fundedYearCostBefore = fundedYearQtyBefore × costPerBond`
- `excessQtyBefore = heldQty − fundedYearQtyBefore` (floored at 0 — a DARA entered above the year's own raw ARA is the user's prerogative, not an error, but excess never goes negative)
- `excessCostBefore = excessQtyBefore × costPerBond`

This makes the flagged year's funded row and Gap sub-row show internally consistent qty/cost/amount, matching how the real engine already presents a resolved funded/excess split after Run — the pre-Run guess should look like a preview of that, not a different shape.

**Scope: preview only.** This flagging/prompting logic is purely a pre-Run display aid. It has no effect on the real duration-match/self-financing engine at Run time — Rebalance Ladder still reads whatever per-year DARA is on the form (the same `daraByYear` map the engine already consumes today, §Per-Year DARA (`daraByYear`) below), completely independent of how the preview displayed things before Run.

#### Funding the rebalance — shape-preserving self-financing scale (Run)

A rebalance must be **self-financing**: net cash (`costDeltaSum`) is a small **non-negative** number — the proceeds of what is sold cover what is bought, with no external cash added. The duration-match bracket excess (sized from the gap block's total cost, see 2.0 §Excess Quantities) is the purchase that must be funded; the cash to buy it is raised by **selling proportionally across the funded rungs**, scaling the entire per-year shape down by a single factor so the ladder keeps its (possibly uneven) shape.

This is **one coupled solve, not a conditional step**, because the inputs are interdependent: the gap-year DARA is the funded-year median, the gap block's total cost depends on that median, the excess to buy depends on the cost, and the cash raised (hence the scale factor) depends on the excess. A **binary search on a single DARA level** dissolves the loop: at each trial level `L` the whole shape is rebuilt at `k = L / median` (every rung × `k`; gap years track the scaled median), the rebalance is run, and `L` is driven to the **highest value at which `costDeltaSum ≥ 0`**. The result is the self-financing scaled per-year map fed to the executed rebalance.

- **Implementation:** `inferScaledDARAFromPortfolio` with `flat = false` (shape-preserving). This is the **single source** for the solve — the same function the selection-toolbar **Infer DARA** action calls (§Per-Rung DARA Selection; it uses `flat = true`, a flat shape; Run uses `flat = false`, the natural shape). Wired into the standard Run path; the displayed panel is **not** rewritten (the user keeps seeing the mirrored/edited shape).
- **The scale is applied ONLY when there is a gap-year / Future-30Y block to fund.** The scale exists solely to raise cash for the duration-match excess those blocks require. Run first executes the rebalance on the per-year store directly; **only if the engine reports gap years or Future-30Y years in range** (`summary.gapYears` / `summary.future30yYears` non-empty) **and** the store is the untouched load mirror is the shape-preserving scale applied (re-run with the scaled map). With no such block there is nothing to buy, so the direct run already nets to ≈ 0 and **no scaling is done** — the held rungs are untouched. (Gating off the engine's own summary avoids re-deriving the ladder range in the UI.) This whole direct-run → conditional-scale orchestration is the library export **`runFundedRebalance`** (single home, unit-tested); the UI shell just passes the per-year store, the `isPristineMirror` flag, and the form values.
  - **Why the gate is necessary (not just an optimization):** the scaled solve recovers its shape from the **held** funded years only; ordinary **empty interior years** (a maturity the user simply holds none of — *not* a structural gap year) are absent from that map and would fall back to the **scalar** DARA inside `runRebalance`, which sizes them as full rungs and emits spurious BUYs. The self-financing search then reads those phantom buys as "excess to fund" and sells the entire ladder down to raise the cash — large, wrong trades on a portfolio that needed none. An **empty interior funded year is an intentional hole** (the user holds none on purpose); the rebalance **never auto-fills it**. The load mirror already encodes this correctly (the empty year carries its incoming LMI, "not a full rung" — see §Per-Year DARA from Portfolio step 1), so running the store directly is a true no-op.

#### Intentional empty rungs and the rung-validation waiver

An **empty interior funded year is an intentional hole by default** — but it is a *default*, not a hard floor. The rule is per-year and provenance-free: the year's **own per-year DARA** decides.

- **Untouched (mirror) empty year** — its DARA is its incoming-LMI stub, so `round((DARA − LMI) / piPerBond)` lands at **0**: a hole, no trade. This is the no-op.
- **Explicitly raised empty year** — when the user lifts that year's DARA above its incoming LMI by at least one bond's worth (e.g. selecting a range spanning the empty year and running Infer DARA or Set DARA — §Per-Rung DARA Selection — or a manual edit), the year sizes to **≥ 1 bond and fills normally**. The panel value is the user's stated intent and is honored — there is no separate "is this a hole" flag.

Because of the first case, `sizeLadder`'s "every funded year must fund at least one bond" validation (which throws `daraTooLowYear`) is **waived for the rebalance's unheld in-range years** (`optionalYears`): an empty year whose target rounds to 0 is a hole, not a too-low-DARA error. The waiver only matters at the knife-edge (an empty year's mirror DARA ≈ its own incoming LMI, which can round either side of the prelim-LMI estimate); a year the user genuinely funds has target ≥ 1 and never reaches the throw. Build passes no `optionalYears` — every build year is a required rung, so its too-low-DARA error stands.
- **Scope — default mirror only.** The Run-time scale applies when the per-year store is the **as-loaded mirror** (unmodified). Once the user has explicitly reshaped it (per-rung edits, or a selection Set DARA / Infer DARA — §Per-Rung DARA Selection), that shape is the user's stated intent and is honored **as-is** — selection Infer already self-finances the years it touches. Files carrying an explicit `#fundedYear,dara` block (our own exports) are likewise honored exactly — **no Run-time scale** — so the build→export→import round-trip stays zero-trade (see 2.1 Broker Import).
- **Relationship to 2.0:** this rule sits entirely above 2.0. 2.0 §Algorithm *sizes* each rung from its DARA and credits bracket-excess coupon down as LMI; it says nothing about cash balancing. The self-financing scale chooses the one DARA level at which 2.0's sizing nets to non-negative cash. No 2.0 claim is altered.

#### Scalar DARA Field

The main DARA field is the fallback for funded years that have no per-year entry. If the user types a new scalar value, it applies to all years not overridden in the per-year panel. If the field is blank, the UI uses `_daraByYearPanelDefault` (set at file load from the portfolio median).

**The field displays blank (with a "by year" placeholder hint) whenever a single number would be
misleading** — any variable per-year shape. It shows the real number only for a genuinely flat
ladder. This keeps the top box and the per-year table from disagreeing with each other —
`_daraByYearPanelDefault` (the internal fallback the box's number comes from) tracks the live
per-year store's own median whenever the box is blank, rather than a stale figure left over from
before a selection Infer/Set DARA or a First/Last Year change. Typing a new number into the box
while it's blank starts from nothing, not a resurrected old flat value the user didn't ask to bring
back; an invalid entry (e.g. below $1,000) is left on screen exactly as typed until Run rejects it,
rather than being silently blanked out from under the user before they see why.

#### Re-run Behaviour

On every Rebalance Ladder click, `_daraByYearPanelDefault` is synced to `finalDara` before `getDaraByYear()` is called, ensuring stored per-year values are applied correctly regardless of whether the panel is visible.

### Pre-Ladder Interest (PLI) in Rebalance

When `preLadderInterest = true`, the pre-ladder pool, its zeroing, and its partial-credit
distribution are computed by the **shared `sizeLadder`** (ladder-core.js) — the *same* function build
calls — not by a rebalance-local copy. Rebalance derives the canonical bond set with
`selectLadderBonds(firstYear, lastYear)`, runs `sizeLadder`, and consumes its
`zeroedFundedYears`, `partialCreditYear`, `partialCredit`, `pliCreditByGapYear`, and
`pliCreditByFundedYear`. The pre-ladder pool is sized from a canonical (DARA-based) prelim sweep over
`[firstYear, lastYear]`; `preLadderPool = preLadderYears × totalAnnualInt + preLadderAmdPool`,
distributed short→long with gap years consuming credit before longer funded years.

**Why this matters (history)**: rebalance previously hand-wrote its own prelim sweep + pool, which
drifted from build's at the partial-credit boundary year (2040) — build credited ~$11k there,
rebalance ~$4.3k, yielding a 4-bond / ~$6k discrepancy on a build→rebalance round-trip. Sourcing the
PLI distribution from `sizeLadder` makes the round-trip produce **zero trades / zero net cash**.

### Build ≡ Rebalance "Before" and "After" (unification invariant)

The rebalance **"Amount After"** for every funded year must equal **build's "Amount"** for that year,
and — for a no-trade round-trip of *our own* file — the **"Amount Before"** must equal it too (the held
ladder IS the target). **Every** build funded year must render in the rebalance table. This is the
contract that makes a build → export → import → rebalance round-trip exact, and it is locked by
tests/run.js ("rebal After == build amount per year" **and** "rebal Before == build amount per year",
worst $0 across flat / +PLI / variable / variable+PLI). Three mechanisms enforce it:

**Scope: single-TIPS-per-year builds (`maturityPref='last'`).** The zero-trade round-trip is
guaranteed for the default `maturityPref='last'` build. Rebalance restores an imported file's
`maturityPref` from the `#params` line, but its **target** side still assumes one target CUSIP per
funded year (`bst.targetCUSIP`); it does not yet reconstruct a multi-TIPS target for a
`semiannual`/`all` build (see 2.0 §Maturity Selection Within a Funded Year, 4.0 §ladder-core.js).
`couponPref` is **never** restored on the rebalance side at all — it is Build-only, so a build with
`couponPref='lower'` combined with a non-`last` `maturityPref` round-trips through rebalance using
the `'higher'` default, which can select different same-month TIPS than the build actually holds.
See §Target CUSIP resolution (Within-Year Allocation Policy) below for how the *single* target CUSIP
is chosen from the (possibly multi-TIPS) candidate set each run.

1. **One shared Amount rule — `ladder-core.fundedYearAmount`.** The per-year Amount (and its
   pre-ladder credit) is computed by a *single* exported function that `build-lib.js` **and both** the
   rebalance "Before" and "After" sweeps call — there is no second copy of the formula to drift. It returns
   `amount = principal + ownCoupon + laterMatInt + ownExcessCoupon + AMD + rollCoupon + credit` (where
   `rollCoupon` is the Future-30Y cover-roll coupon credited to 2053–56; see 2.0 §Cover-roll coupon), where for a fully
   PLI-funded (zeroed) year `credit = max(0, DARA_y − fixedIncome)` (reconciled so the row lands exactly
   on its DARA) and otherwise `credit = partialCredit` (the year's non-zeroed pre-ladder share, else 0).
   This matters because `sizeLadder` sizes the PLI pool against the *preliminary* later-mat interest
   (the approximation that decides *which* years zero), so `pliCreditByFundedYear[y]` is a prelim-basis
   credit, whereas a row's ARA is built from the *corrected* components (`lAfter` + own-year excess
   coupon `exIntA` + AMD); the shared rule reconciles the zeroed-year credit to those corrected
   components. Without it a zeroed year overshot its DARA by `(lAfter − prelimLMI)` (e.g. 2034 showing
   $22,957 against a $20,000 DARA under variable DARA + PLI). A *non-zeroed* year that later-maturity
   interest alone over-covers legitimately exceeds its DARA (e.g. 2035 → $31,790 vs a $30,000 DARA) —
   **build shows the same value**; that is not an overshoot, the rung simply needs no bonds.
   Display-only: trades use the sized quantities.

2. **Render fully-covered rungs.** Rebalance emits "After" rows from holdings + buys, so a ladder rung
   that is fully covered (zeroed by PLI, or over-covered by LMI) — hence has no holding *and* no buy —
   would silently vanish (the "skipped 2035" bug). After the holdings/buy rows, a final pass over the
   canonical `rangeYears` emits any missing rung as an informational row (`qtyBefore = qtyAfter = 0`,
   Amount After = `postARAByYear[y]`), mirroring build, which always renders every ladder year.

   The same vanishing failure mode recurs at CUSIP granularity: a synthetic-row pass emits a buy into
   `targetCUSIP` when that CUSIP has no current holdings (e.g. 3-bracket `newLowerYear`, or an ordinary
   `'all'`/`'saYield'` year whose top-ranked candidate is a brand-new CUSIP never held before). This is
   gated on **the target CUSIP specifically**, not on "this funded year has zero holdings of any kind"
   — a year can already have other held CUSIPs (already emitted via the main holdings loop) while the
   target itself is still a fresh buy that loop never sees. Gating on "any holdings" let that buy
   vanish from the table/Trade Ticket/export while still being counted in the year's Amount After total
   — Amount After would grow with no visible trade to explain it.

3. **"Before" runs the same sweep with held quantities.** "Amount Before" is the current-holdings
   valuation, so it uses *held* qtys — but through the **same** `fundedYearAmount` rule as After, with
   the same component set: own principal + own coupon + LMI + **held excess coupon** (`exIntB`) + AMD +
   credit. Previously `beforeARAByYear` was a separate, cruder sum (`pB + cB + lBefore + amdBefore`)
   that omitted the bracket excess coupon and the pre-ladder credit and over-scaled AMD — so Before
   drifted from build even with zero trades (20k+ deficits at zeroed/bracket years like 2036/2040, and
   ~$500/yr from the AMD basis). The pre-ladder **credit in Before is applied only for our own files**
   (those carrying an explicit per-year DARA block); broker files (inferred DARA) keep their existing
   Before semantics until broker handling is revisited. For a broker file *with* trades, Before ≠ After
   legitimately — that difference is the point of "Before". **Bracket display:** a designated gap
   bracket renders with `*` + a qty-0 excess sub-row even at 0 excess, in **both** build and rebalance
   (`render.js isBracket` uses `excess > 0 || isGapBracket` for both modes) — previously rebalance hid a
   0-excess bracket (e.g. 2036/2040 under high later-year DARA), while build showed it.

**AMD-inclusive DARA inference**: when `dara = null`, the inferred DARA is the median of each
funded year's `P+I + LMI + held-2052 AMD + Future-30Y roll coupon`. Because Option C credits held-to-maturity
2052 AMD (and, for 2053–56, the cover-roll coupon) to every funded year's DARA, the raw `P+I + LMI` median
understates DARA; adding the AMD **and** roll coupon the portfolio actually earns (both scaled by held 2052
excess) recovers the true DARA. The natural-ARA recovery (`inferScaledDARAFromPortfolio`) likewise adds back
`ownExcessCoupon + AMD + rollCoupon` at the cover/post-cover years so the recovered map matches build's identity.

### Per-Year DARA (`daraByYear`)

An optional `Map<year, number>` allowing any ladder year — funded, gap, or Future 30Y — to target a different annual real amount. When provided:

- Each funded year in Phase 4 uses `daraByYear.get(year) ?? DARA` as its target — years absent from the map fall back to the scalar DARA
- Gap years use `daraByYear.get(year) ?? DARA` in the synthetic-qty formula and in the PLI-pool need estimate; a higher gap-year DARA increases synthetic gap quantities, which raises `gapParams.totalCost` and therefore increases bracket excess quantities
- Future 30Y years use `daraByYear.get(year) ?? DARA` in the hypothetical-qty formula; a higher future-30Y DARA increases `future30yTotalCost` and therefore increases future 30Y cover excess quantities
- Bracket target funded-year qty is computed with the bracket year's own DARA from the map
- When `daraByYear` is null/absent, behaviour is identical to the scalar-DARA path

**This rule applies to the 2040 upper-bracket target and every 2041+ rung feeding its LMI
estimate, not just the funded years a caller sizes directly.** The gap-parameter calculation's
preliminary long→short sweep (which estimates the income arriving at 2040 from all rungs above it,
to size the 2040/gap bracket excess) and the 2040 target-quantity itself must read
`daraByYear.get(year) ?? DARA` per year, the same as everywhere else — not the scalar `DARA` alone.
Previously they did not: a stale scalar (e.g. the UI's top-level DARA box left out of sync with a
freshly-inferred per-year shape) would size the bracket excess for
a materially different, wrong income level even though the funded years below were sized correctly
from the real per-year map — leaving a large, uncorrected net-cash surplus after Run (traced case:
$21,417/yr real per-year target vs. a stale $41,500 scalar leaking into the bracket sizing produced
a $9,186 surplus; fixing the scalar sync alone got it to $1,031, fixing this rule is what makes that
$1,031-scale residual the general case regardless of UI staleness). The fix generalizes past this
one bug: *any* caller supplying a
non-flat `daraByYear` depends on the gap/bracket math actually reading it for every rung, not just
the ones it sizes by name.

### Per-Rung DARA Selection

> **Terminology note — LMI+AMD.** "LMI" (later-maturity interest) is now an incomplete name:
> when the ladder extends past 2056 the held excess 2052 TIPS contribute **accrued market
> discount (AMD)** that is credited to earlier years as interest. The income flowing downward
> in the sweep is therefore **LMI+AMD** (later-maturity interest *and* accrued market discount).
> The selection model below depends on this downward flow.

Earlier revisions of this app split the ladder at explicit, persisted **split years** into named
segments, each with its own Infer/constant-stamp UI in a separate DARA Plan card. That conflated
two distinct things: a convenience for assigning one DARA value to a run of rungs (genuinely
useful — see §Two Distinct Actions below), and a permanent boundary that had to be created,
persisted, exported, and re-detected from holdings holes. The boundary added ceremony without
adding information the CUSIP/Qty export doesn't already carry (the resulting per-year DARA values
*are* the durable plan). Split years, segments, the DARA Plan card, and the holdings-hole
auto-split detection have all been removed in favor of acting directly on the ladder table.

#### Selecting rungs

A **selection** is a transient set of funded years chosen directly on the ladder table (Rebalance's
`#simple-table` or Build's `#build-table`), never persisted beyond the current session:

- **Click** a funded-year group-header row — toggles that row's expand/collapse (unchanged
  behavior) **and** selects that one row (the anchor for a following Shift-click).
- **Shift-click** a second row — selects the whole range between the last-clicked row and this one.
- **Click-and-drag** across rows — builds the same range selection live as the pointer moves; the
  trailing click this produces is suppressed (it just finished selecting, not toggling
  expand/collapse).

The selection drives a small toolbar (`#selection-toolbar`) that appears only while at least one
rung is selected, with a year-range label, a **Set DARA** input + Apply, an **Infer DARA** button
(Rebalance only — Build has no holdings to self-finance against), and a Clear button. Switching
mode (Build ↔ Rebalance) or loading a new file clears the current selection.

#### Two distinct actions

- **Set DARA** (`_runSelectionSetDara`, both modes) — stamps one typed value across every currently
  selected rung, marks each as manually pinned (`_daraManualYears`, same as a hand-typed per-rung
  edit), then cascades below (see below).
- **Infer DARA** (`_runSelectionInfer`, **Rebalance only**) — a scoped self-financing solve
  (`inferScaledDARAFromPortfolio`, `flat = true`) over the selected years alone, against current
  holdings, then cascades below. Build has no holdings to solve against, so its toolbar never shows
  this button.

Both actions respect **manual-year pins**: a year the user typed directly into its own row is held
fixed within the selection — its real cash flow still counts toward the self-finance target, it
just isn't a candidate for restamping.

#### Why a cascade below the selection (and not an isolated solve)

LMI+AMD flows **only downward** (longer → shorter maturities), so changing a selection's DARA
changes the income arriving at every year below it, which changes what those years need. Ignoring
that would leave the years below stale and silently drift the whole ladder away from
self-financing. So both actions above **cascade**: immediately after the selection's own change,
every year from `firstYear` up to (not including) the selection's own minimum year is re-solved as
one flat self-financing scope (`_cascadeReinferBelow`) — the years above the selection are never
touched (income never flows upward). This reproduces net whole-portfolio cash ≈ 0 without the user
having to remember to walk back down and re-click anything.

`inferScaledDARAFromPortfolio` (the scoped solve both the selection Infer and the cascade call) is
unchanged from its scoped-segment form: `scopeYears` are solved for, years outside are held fixed
at `fixedDaraByYear`, and `pinnedDaraByYear` keeps manually-typed years fixed within scope while
still counting their cash flow. Trades outside `[firstYear, lastYear]` (e.g. the upper bracket when
`lastYear` lands inside the structural gap) are still credited to whichever scope reaches
`lastYearOverride`, via `inCashScope`, same as before.

#### No edit history

There is no Undo or Revert-to-loaded — both were removed once editing became this cheap: a
selection covers as many rungs as needed in one action, so walking back a mistake is just making
another selection and setting or inferring the correct value, the same way the original edit was
made. `_daraLoadedSnapshot` (the as-loaded/imported map, per-mode) still exists internally, but only
to let `_storeIsPristineMirror()` (§Funding the rebalance above) tell whether the store has been
touched since load — it has no user-facing restore path.

#### Persisting a DARA plan across sessions

File load always **mirrors current holdings with no inference** (§Per-Year DARA from Portfolio), so
re-uploading a fresh broker export (Vanguard/Fidelity/Schwab) wipes any per-year shape set up on a
prior visit — brokers never carry the `#fundedYear,dara` block that makes *our own* exports
reproduce exactly (2.1 Broker Import), and a broker file has no DARA data of its own to overlay a
plan onto. The **standalone DARA-plan file** (2.1 §Standalone DARA-plan file — plain Export/Import
buttons in the main card, no CUSIP rows, no segments) is the durable, portable path for this:
export the current per-year shape once, and re-import it onto any subsequent fresh holdings upload,
on any device. There is no same-device localStorage cache anymore — it was found to surface an
unwanted "saved plan found" prompt on nearly every load and was dropped; the standalone file is now
the only persistence mechanism, used deliberately rather than offered automatically.

## Named Quantities & Formulas (Rebalance)

### Target CUSIP resolution (Within-Year Allocation Policy)

For each ordinary (non-bracket) funded year in Phase 4, before any of the quantity formulas below
apply, `rankForYear({ candidates: _canon.yearTipsListMap[year], held: yi.holdings, policy:
allocationPolicy, maturityPref, rankOverride: yearRankOverrides.get(year) })` (2.0 §Within-Year
Allocation Policy, 4.0 §allocation-policy.js) ranks that year's candidate ∪ held union, highest
buy/hold-priority first:

```
targetCUSIP = rank[0].cusip
nonTarget   = rank.slice(1), filtered to currently-held cusips
```

**Sell order (`'maturity'`/`'saYield'`, or an active per-year override).** `nonTarget`, in rank order
(least-preferred-to-hold first), is drained one at a time — fully settling each to zero before moving
to the next — until the year's own P+I `need` is met or `nonTarget` is exhausted; the drain **stops
the instant a CUSIP isn't fully drained**, leaving every less-preferred-to-sell CUSIP after it
(including `targetCUSIP`) untouched, even if one of them would arithmetically divide the leftover
residual more evenly (a smaller per-bond value one rank later must never let it sell ahead of an
earlier, still-held CUSIP). Only once every `nonTarget` member has been sold to zero does the
remaining `need` gap — deficit (buy) or a residual too small to be met by any further whole-bond sale
(shrink) — apply to `targetCUSIP`; while any `nonTarget` member still holds qty, that residual is
accepted as a small under/over-shoot rather than touching `targetCUSIP`. This is a straightforward
strengthening of the pre-feature rule (previously: always target the latest-maturing held CUSIP,
always sell oldest-held-first) — the same one-CUSIP-at-a-time drain shape, now driven by a ranking
that reflects the active policy instead of a hardcoded direction.

**Sell order (`'equal'`)** does not drain one CUSIP fully before the next — see 2.0 §Within-Year
Allocation Policy's `levelValues()` description. The target/non-target pool (the same set as above)
is leveled toward equal value together: growth water-fills the currently-lowest-value member(s),
shrinkage drains the currently-highest-value member(s), re-leveling at every step. `targetCUSIP`
still absorbs whatever rounding remainder is left once every other pool member lands on its leveled
value — the same remainder-absorption pattern §Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split already uses for a
build's last sub-rung.

**Scope.** Bracket/gap/future30y years never call `rankForYear` — their target CUSIP is identified by
§Bracket Identification Rules / 2.0 §Duration Matching instead, and their sell order (when Retained
Bracket Excess applies) is the oldest-first rule in 2.0 §Retained Bracket Excess.

**Funded-Year Quantity Before, raw estimate** (`fyQtyBeforeRaw`):
```
fyQtyBeforeRaw_b = ROUND((DARA − LMIFromAbove_Before − nonTargetPI) / piPerBond_b)
```
`LMIFromAbove_Before` = sum of annual interest from all actual holdings in years maturing after year b (includes both funded and excess holdings at those years). `nonTargetPI` = P+I from non-target CUSIPs held in the same bracket year. Future30y cover years are excluded — treated as pure excess (`fyQtyBeforeRaw = 0`). This formula is applied to ALL standard gap bracket years regardless of CSV format. This is a pure holdings-interpretation estimate — it must never reference `fyQtyAfter` or any other after-side target — but it is a *raw estimate*, not yet the displayed Quantity Before (see reconciliation below).

**Excess Quantity Before, raw estimate** (`excessQtyBeforeRaw`):
```
excessQtyBeforeRaw_b = MAX(0, qtyBefore_b − fyQtyBeforeRaw_b)
```
Funded-first rule: apply the funded-year claim first; the remainder of the held quantity is the current excess. This *derives* the before-split rather than reading the import's excess column back verbatim — but the **imported excess is not discarded**. It still does its two jobs: in duration matching it is the cover/bracket excess (the recorded build intent), and in funded sizing its annual interest and realized AMD role-play as later-maturity interest (`LMIFromAbove_Before` above counts both funded **and** excess holdings). The *size* of the imported cover excess is also what recovers the intended first and last funded year (see 2.0 §Gap Year Coverage Model and §Future 30Y Rungs). Because a correctly-built ladder's derived split reproduces its own imported excess, a build→export→import→rebalance round-trip yields no trades. For Future-30Y cover years: `excessQtyBeforeRaw = ROUND(future30yTotalCost × weight_b / costPerBond_b)` (cover-pair cost split).

**Excess Quantity After** (`excessQtyAfter`):
```
excessQtyAfter_b = ROUND(totalBlockCost × weight_b / costPerBond_b)
```
Post-rebalance excess for duration matching. `costPerBond = price/100 × indexRatio × 1000` (per **TIPS Basics**).

**Reconciling the raw split with the after target (no same-maturity buy+sell).**
`fyQtyBeforeRaw`/`excessQtyBeforeRaw` above are computed purely from holdings — they never reference
`fyQtyAfter`/`excessQtyAfter`. But they are independently-derived *estimates*, using the before-side
LMI cascade and this year's own before-side DARA math, and routinely disagree with the after-side
targets (`fyQtyAfter_b`, `excessQtyAfter_b`) even at an otherwise-untouched bracket year — rebalancing
elsewhere in the ladder shifts the later-maturity-interest cascade feeding this year's own funded
formula, and a custom per-year DARA plan can push the after-side funded need further still. A bracket-
target CUSIP's funded and excess portions are the *same held maturity*, so a plain per-bucket
subtraction of the raw estimate from After is only wrong when the two buckets' naive deltas point in
*opposite* directions — one bucket would buy this maturity while the other sells it, a nonsensical
simultaneous buy+sell of one bond. When both buckets move the same direction (or one doesn't move at
all), the raw estimate already reconciles `fyQtyBeforeRaw_b + Δ = fyQtyAfter_b` per bucket on its own
and passes straight through unchanged:

```
naiveFyDelta_b     = fyQtyAfter_b − fyQtyBeforeRaw_b
naiveExcessDelta_b = excessQtyAfter_b − excessQtyBeforeRaw_b

if naiveFyDelta_b and naiveExcessDelta_b have OPPOSITE signs:
    realloc_b        = MIN(|naiveFyDelta_b|, |naiveExcessDelta_b|)   // signed toward whichever is buying
    fyQtyBefore_b     = fyQtyBeforeRaw_b + realloc_b       (moving toward the buying bucket)
    excessQtyBefore_b = excessQtyBeforeRaw_b − realloc_b
else:
    fyQtyBefore_b     = fyQtyBeforeRaw_b
    excessQtyBefore_b = excessQtyBeforeRaw_b

fyQtyDelta_b     = fyQtyAfter_b − fyQtyBefore_b
excessQtyDelta_b = excessQtyAfter_b − excessQtyBefore_b
```
Relabeling currently-held units between the two buckets costs nothing and trades nothing — only the
opposing portion is netted out this way; before pricing any trade, as much of what's already held as
will cancel the conflicting signs is relabeled first, and only the remaining shortfall or leftover is
actually bought or sold. **`fyQtyBefore`/`excessQtyBefore` — the reconciled values, not the raw
estimates — are what render as the Quantity Before / Cost Before / Excess Amount Before columns.**
Showing the raw estimate instead (e.g. displaying `fyQtyBeforeRaw_b` as "Quantity Before" while the
delta is computed against the reconciled `fyQtyBefore_b`) was tried and rejected: it reintroduces the
exact arithmetic break this reconciliation exists to prevent (Before + Delta ≠ After on-screen), since
whatever number is *displayed* as Before must be the same number the delta is computed against. In the
(usual) same-direction case the two coincide exactly, so nothing changes there — this only alters the
displayed split in the rarer opposite-sign case, and even then only by relabeling units already held
(the total `qtyBefore_b` is untouched).

This guarantees three invariants always hold: (1) `fyQtyDelta_b` and `excessQtyDelta_b` are never
opposite in sign for the same CUSIP; (2) `fyQtyDelta_b + excessQtyDelta_b = qtyAfter_b − qtyBefore_b`
(the reallocation only changes how the total trade is split between the two buckets, never the total
itself); (3) `fyQtyBefore_b + fyQtyDelta_b = fyQtyAfter_b` and `excessQtyBefore_b + excessQtyDelta_b =
excessQtyAfter_b` — Before + Delta = After reconciles per bucket, not just in total.

**Lower-side priority rule** (`bracketWeightsN`, 4.0 §Computation Modules; replaces the plain
two-sided formula across all lower-side maturities; see 2.0 §Retained Bracket Excess). Retained
maturities are **not** priced as though they sat at the active lower bracket's duration — each is
frozen at its own (shorter) duration, and only the active lower bracket and upper bracket are
solved for, from the duration-match equation with the retained legs as fixed inputs:

```
R   = Σ (retainedExcessCost_r) / totalBlockCost
D_R = Σ (retainedExcessCost_r / totalBlockCost) × dur_r

upperWeight       = ((dGap − D_R) − (1 − R) × dur_activeLower) / (dur_upper − dur_activeLower)
activeLowerWeight = (1 − R) − upperWeight
```

**`activeFloorWeight` — the active lower bracket's floor.** The active lower bracket is "the only
lower-side maturity a rebalance buys" ([Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket))
— it is never sold to make room for a duration match the way a retained leg may be. `bracketWeightsN`
takes `activeFloorWeight` (default 0): the active bracket's own currently-held excess, as a share of
`totalBlockCost`. Every trigger and target below that reads "0" is really this floor — a plain 0 only
when the active bracket currently holds no excess of its own, which is why every pre-existing caller
that omits `activeFloorWeight` reproduces the exact original math unchanged.

**Why this matters (traced case).** A large, short-duration retained leg can pull the *unconstrained*
solve's `activeLowerWeight` down toward — but not past — literal zero without ever going negative. The
original rule ("sell retained only when `activeLowerWeight` solves negative") missed this: the active
bracket's excess was treated as pure target cost and sold down to whatever the unconstrained weight
computed, which could be **less** than what it already held — backwards from the intent. A real
2034/2036 portfolio hit this exactly: Jan 2034 (retained, duration 6.93, holding a disproportionately
large lower-side excess share) computed an active-bracket weight of ~3×10⁻¹⁶ (float noise for exactly
0, never negative) for Jan 2036 (active), so the "over-allocated, sell retained" branch never
triggered — the code instead took the "under-allocated, freeze retained" branch and sold 2036's excess
from 36 bonds to 0 while 2034 kept 75 of its 83. Fixed by flooring the solve at `activeFloorWeight`.

If `activeLowerWeight` solves below `activeFloorWeight` (**over-allocated** — the lower side is
carrying more than the block needs, even leaving room for the active bracket's own current holding):
- Sell the **earliest** [retained bracket excess](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#retained-bracket-excess)
  maturity, and **only as much of it as restoring the match requires** — solve for the exact
  weight of that leg that brings `activeLowerWeight` back to exactly `activeFloorWeight`, holding
  every other retained leg fixed, and clamp to `[0, currently held]`. Selling the whole leg when a
  partial sale would do is needless trading, not the rule.
- Only if depleting that leg **entirely** is still not enough does the next-earliest retained
  maturity get the same treatment.
- Retained excess is **never bought**; the active lower bracket and upper bracket absorb whatever
  the retained legs no longer cover — the active bracket never below its own floor.

If `activeLowerWeight` solves at or above `activeFloorWeight` on the first pass (**under-allocated**):
- Freeze every retained maturity at its current excess (never increased): `targetCost_b = excessCost_b`
- Buy the active lower bracket to fill the remainder: `targetCost_activeLower = totalBlockCost × activeLowerWeight`
  (never below `totalBlockCost × activeFloorWeight`, i.e. never a net sell of the active bracket).

For each lower-side maturity `b`: `excessQtyAfter_b = ROUND(targetCost_b / costPerBond_b)`

With no retained maturities (`R = 0`) this reduces to the plain two-sided case: the active lower
bracket is sized straight from `bracketWeights(dur_activeLower, dur_upper, dGap)`.

**Round-trip rounding note** (2.0 §Retained Bracket Excess): the over-allocated partial-sell target
is a continuous dollar figure; `ROUND()`-ing it to a whole bond can land the holding a few dollars
short. Exporting and reimporting that portfolio unchanged can therefore ask for a very small
follow-up trade — up to about one bond, always bought into the active lower bracket, never back
into retained excess — on the very next rebalance. Accepted as an ordinary whole-lot-investing
artifact, not a bug; the test suite tolerates it (≤1 bond of churn) rather than requiring
bit-perfect round-trip idempotency in this specific regime.

**Cash Delta** (`cashDelta`, `costDelta`):
```
cashDelta = -(qtyDelta × costPerTips)
  where qtyDelta = qtyAfter - qtyBefore
```
Positive = cash received (net sell); negative = cash outflow (net buy). At a bracket-target CUSIP,
the funded-row and excess-row Cash Deltas use `fyQtyDelta`/`excessQtyDelta` (the reallocated split
above), not a raw `fyQtyAfter − fyQtyBefore` / `excessQtyAfter − excessQtyBefore` subtraction — the
latter can manufacture a same-CUSIP buy+sell pair. The row total (`qtyAfter − qtyBefore`) is
unaffected either way; only its split between the two rows changes.

## Bracket Identification Rules

The active lower bracket comes from `tipsMap`; retained maturities come from holdings.

### Active Lower Bracket (Canonical)
The [active lower bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket) is **always** the most recently issued ladder-eligible 10-year TIPS from `tipsMap` maturing below `minGapYear` — the longest-dated 10-year maturity available (Jan 2036 as of 2026-07-27; Jul 2036 on issuance). Holdings are not consulted. This is the same search used for `anchorBefore` in gap parameter calculation. When **Retain lower bracket excess** is off, older holdings that previously served as the lower bracket are no longer in `bracketYearSet`; Full rebalance rebuilds them to funded-year qty only, selling any excess.

### Retained Maturities (Holdings-Based)
When **Retain lower bracket excess** is on, `identifyBrackets` (`rebalance-lib.js`) picks a **single** orig-lower CUSIP from holdings, older than the active lower bracket:
1.  **CUSIP Aggregation**: Sum total quantity of each CUSIP across the entire portfolio.
2.  **Excess ARA Priority**: The primary metric is **Excess ARA** (`araByYear[y] - DARA`). Among candidate years, the **single** year with the highest Excess ARA is picked as the orig-lower retained maturity. **Note**: "Excess ARA" is a one-time empirical probe used solely here — not a general concept.
3.  **Tie-Breaking**: Among candidates with equal Excess ARA, pick the CUSIP with the highest aggregate quantity.
4.  **Range**: Only years in `[LOWEST_LOWER_BRACKET_YEAR (2032), minGapYear)` are considered.
5.  **Fallback**: If no candidate year is found (e.g. `firstYear` inside the gap), only the active lower bracket carries lower-side excess — the lower side reduces to the plain two-sided case.

**Currently limited to one generation.** Conceptually any number of retained maturities may accumulate as the active lower bracket rolls forward — each rebalance buys the then-active lower bracket, and when a later maturity is issued the previous one becomes retained (two generations, Jan 2034 alongside Jan 2036, is the common case today; three arrives on the next issuance) — and `bracketWeightsN` (2.0 §Retained Bracket Excess) is written to solve for any number of them. But `identifyBrackets` above only ever returns one orig-lower CUSIP, so only the single most-recent retained generation is fed into the solve today; identifying and ordering multiple retained generations from holdings is a known gap, not yet implemented (see branch `retained-bracket-work` for a prior, reverted attempt and why it broke on real holdings).

## Rebalance Algorithm Flow

This is *the* rebalance algorithm (there is one rebalance — the "Gap-only" mode it was once named for was scrapped; see §Purpose). It runs the full ladder including the gap brackets, and is driven by the **per-year DARA targets**, which may be uneven (2.0 §Duration Matching).

### Phase 1: Derive Parameters
- Settlement date, RefCPI, per-year DARA targets.
- Build holdings ladder and identify official range.
- **DARA shape**: the per-year targets come from the panel store (mirror at load, or user-reshaped). For the default mirror, Run applies the **shape-preserving self-financing scale** (see §Funding the rebalance) so the executed targets net to non-negative cash; an explicitly reshaped store / our-export DARA block is used as-is.
- Identify current gap years.

### Phase 2: Missing Block Parameters
- Calculate **Gap Avg Duration** and **Gap Total Cost** (see **2.0 TIPS Ladders**).
- If `lastYear > 2056`, calculate **Future 30Y Avg Duration** and **Future 30Y Total Cost**.

### Phase 3: Bracket Resolution
- Identify lower/upper bracket CUSIPs from holdings per §Bracket Identification Rules.
- Calculate modified duration for each bracket.
- Solve weights using **2.0 TIPS Ladders**; apply the lower-side priority rule when retained bracket excess is present.
- If `lastYear > 2056`, identify Future 30Y cover pair and solve weights.

### Phase 4: Ladder Rebuild (longest to shortest)
Process ALL years in [firstYear, lastYear] **longest to shortest**.
- **Bracket years**: use BEFORE-state LMI pool to ensure `fyQty` remains constant.
- **Rebalance years**: use running post-rebalance LMI pool.
- Apply bracket excess quantities on top of target `fyQty`.
- **LMI pool rule**: Process according to **2.0 TIPS Ladders §Algorithm**. Excess bracket holdings are actual bonds — their interest reduces funded-year quantities of all shorter rungs, on equal footing with funded-year holdings.

## Excess Balance Check

A **diagnostic** of how well the *current* holdings pre-funded the gap coverage — **not** the funding invariant. The funding invariant is **net cash (`costDeltaSum`) ≥ 0 and small**, guaranteed by the self-financing scale (§Funding the rebalance).
```
1. Previous excess cost
2. Cost to fill rebalance years
3. Gap theoretical cost

Surplus/(Shortfall) = 1 - 2 - 3
```
Near-zero indicates the portfolio already held the bracket excess that the gap requires. **Large negative is expected for a basic file** (broker/legacy CSV) that was never built by this app and so never pre-funded the gap — it simply means the rebalance must buy that excess now. The rebalance still self-finances: the shape-preserving scale sells the funded rungs down to raise the cash, so `costDeltaSum` lands small/non-negative even when this surplus is deeply negative. Read this check as "did the prior portfolio carry its gap coverage," and read `costDeltaSum` as "is the rebalance self-financing."
