# 5.0 Load and Parse Source Data

Specifies process 7.1 of the Yield Curves data flow diagram and the seven processes it decomposes into. Everything the app computes downstream rests on what this stage produces, so the shape of its output is the contract the rest of the app is written against.

## Dependencies

- [DATA_DICTIONARY.md](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md) — the terms used here, [Settlement Date](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date), [Index Ratio](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#index-ratio), [SA Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor), [Clean Price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#clean-price)
- [DataStores.md](../../knowledge/DataStores.md) — the five stores read here: [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1), [S4](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s4), [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7), [S12](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s12), and the bond holidays file
- [1.0 Seasonal Adjustments](./1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) — consumes the SA factors this stage produces
- [4.0 Spot Yield Curves](./4.0_Spot_Yield_Curves.md) — consumes the priced bonds this stage produces

---

## 1.0 What This Stage Is For

The app reads five data stores that share no common shape. Two are CSV with different header conventions, one is CSV without a header, one is JSON. None of them carries a settlement date, and none of them carries a price that can be compared against another store's price without adjustment.

This stage turns those five into one population of **priced bonds**, each carrying the fields every downstream process expects, so that no downstream process has to know which store a bond came from.

Every process below runs in the browser at page load. Nothing here writes a data store.

## 2.0 Processes

<a id="parse-fedinvest-prices"></a>
### 2.1 Parse FedInvest prices (7.1.1)

Reads [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1).

The file is not a plain CSV: its first row is a bare settlement date, its second row is the header, and the data begins on the third. The date row is read first and attached to every row parsed from the rest, because the file carries one settlement date for all of its rows rather than a date per row.

Rows are then split by the `Type` column into TIPS and nominal populations. That split is what lets the app show a nominal curve and a real curve from a single store.

**Output:** priced rows, each with CUSIP, type, maturity, coupon, dated date Ref CPI, price, yield, and the file's settlement date.

<a id="parse-market-quotes"></a>
### 2.2 Parse market quotes (7.1.2)

Reads [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7).

One combined export holding both Treasury and TIPS rows, separated by its `Product` column, with a trailing footer line carrying the download timestamp. The exact header names and the fields used are specified in [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7); they are the export's own names and are parsed verbatim rather than normalised to the Data Dictionary's field names.

The footer timestamp is the file's own trade date. It is extracted here and passed to [2.6](#determine-settlement-dates), because it — not the FedInvest price date — is what the settlement date for these quotes is derived from.

**Output:** bid and ask quotes keyed by CUSIP, and the quote file's trade date.

<a id="parse-ref-cpi-and-sa-factors"></a>
### 2.3 Parse Ref CPI and SA factors (7.1.3)

Reads [S4](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s4).

Yields one row per calendar day carrying the NSA Ref CPI, the SA Ref CPI, and the [SA Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor) between them. Lookup is by exact date: the series carries a row for every calendar day within its published range, so within range there is always an exact match, and outside it there is none. There is no nearest-date fallback.

**Output:** the daily Ref CPI series, and the SA factor lookup that [1.0 Seasonal Adjustments](./1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) consumes.

<a id="parse-bond-holidays"></a>
### 2.4 Parse bond holidays (7.1.4)

Reads the bond holidays file, a headerless CSV of bond market closure dates.

**Output:** the set of dates that are not bond trading days. Used only by [2.6](#determine-settlement-dates), which is the sole reason this store is read.

<a id="parse-gsw-parameters"></a>
### 2.5 Parse GSW parameters (7.1.5)

Reads [S12](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s12), a single record holding the Federal Reserve's own published curve parameters and the date they were fitted for.

Nothing is evaluated here. The parameters pass through to [4.0 Spot Yield Curves](./4.0_Spot_Yield_Curves.md), which evaluates them to draw the published curve as a reference against the app's own fit.

**Output:** the published curve parameters and their observation date.

<a id="determine-settlement-dates"></a>
### 2.6 Determine settlement dates (7.1.6)

Takes the bond trading days from [2.4](#parse-bond-holidays) and each source's own file date, and produces the [Settlement Date](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date) each source's prices are stated at.

**The two sources settle differently, and neither derives from the other.**

- **FedInvest** publishes no settlement date. Its yields are matched empirically by treating the price date as the settlement date, so T = 0 for this source alone.
- **Market quotes** settle at T+1: the quote file's own trade date, from [2.2](#parse-market-quotes), advanced by one bond trading day.

A quote's settlement date is derived from the quote file's trade date. It is never derived from the FedInvest price date: the two sources are downloaded on their own schedules and a broker quote has no relationship to when FedInvest published.

**Output:** one settlement date per source.

<a id="build-priced-bonds"></a>
### 2.7 Build priced bonds (7.1.7)

Combines the parsed rows, the quotes, the daily Ref CPI and the settlement dates into the single population every downstream process reads.

For each bond: the [Index Ratio](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#index-ratio) is computed from the Ref CPI of its source's settlement date over its dated date Ref CPI, and the [Clean Price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#clean-price) is carried alongside it so that a downstream process can work in either real or adjusted terms without re-deriving the ratio.

**Output:** priced bonds, to [1.0 Seasonal Adjustments](./1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) and [4.0 Spot Yield Curves](./4.0_Spot_Yield_Curves.md); bid and ask quotes, to the spread comparison; and each source's dates, to the rendering stage that labels the charts with them.

## 3.0 Known Defect

[2.6](#determine-settlement-dates) does not yet behave as specified above. The settlement date for market-quote TIPS is currently derived from the FedInvest price date rather than from the quote file's own trade date. The single correct derivation exists in the app and is already used for the nominal fit, the breakeven fit and the source labels; the TIPS path has not been moved onto it.

This changes SA, SAO and breakeven figures for TIPS whenever the two sources were downloaded on different days, which is most days.
