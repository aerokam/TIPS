# 2.4 Seasonal Adjustment (SA) Overlay

## Dependencies
[YieldCurves/knowledge/1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md](../../YieldCurves/knowledge/1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) (the SA transform itself — foundational, read first), [YieldCurves/knowledge/2.1_SA_Intuition.md](../../YieldCurves/knowledge/2.1_SA_Intuition.md), [1.0_Operation.md](./1.0_Operation.md) (symbol reliability), [2.1_Time_Series.md](./2.1_Time_Series.md), [2.2_Yield_Curves.md](./2.2_Yield_Curves.md), [2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md](./2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md), [DATA_DICTIONARY.md](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield), [TIPS_Basics.md](../../knowledge/TIPS_Basics.md) (yield calculation conventions).

## Purpose
Adds an optional Seasonally Adjusted (SA) yield overlay to the Time Series tab's TIPS charts, the Yield Curves tab's TIPS curve, and the Breakeven Inflation tab's BEI curve, for all five TIPS maturities the app tracks: **1Y, 2Y, 5Y, 10Y, 30Y**. The seasonal effect amortizes with maturity and is at or below noise level by 10Y/30Y (see YieldCurves/knowledge/2.2_SAO_Residual_Analysis.md) — the SA line there sits almost on top of the raw line — but it's still computed and shown at those maturities for curve completeness, rather than leaving the Yield Curves/BEI overlays visibly stop at 5Y.

## The key fact this feature depends on
CNBC's `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS`/`US5YTIPS`/`US10YTIPS`/`US30YTIPS` symbols are **not** synthetic constant-maturity indices — each is the bid yield of one specific, real TIPS. CNBC's own quote page identifies which one (e.g. `US2YTIPS` = the Jan 2028 0.50% TIPS as of 2026-07-01). This means the exact same Price → SA Price → SA Yield transform YieldCurves applies to actual TIPS applies here directly, using that bond's real coupon and maturity date — no synthetic-bond construction is needed or used.

## Bond identity: today vs. history
- **Today's point**: uses CNBC's live quote REST endpoint (`quote.cnbc.com/quote-html-webservice/restQuote/symbolType/symbol`, batched with `|`-separated symbols) for `maturity_date` and `coupon` directly — empirically confirmed to match the actual quoted price (`bond_last_price`) to the cent.
- **Every other (historical) point**: CNBC only exposes each symbol's *current* underlying bond, not a historical CUSIP-per-date mapping. The bond identity instead comes from `SA_ROLLOVER_LOG` (`src/app.js`) — an observed record of which maturity each symbol actually quoted on which dates — joined against `TipsRef.csv` (S2, the full TIPS auction record) for that maturity's coupon.

## The rollover log
An earlier version of this feature assumed CNBC re-picks the underlying bond on a fixed calendar rule — every 6 months, on the 15th of the tenor's origin-auction month, always the same family (`US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS`: 10-year note, Jan/Jul; `US5YTIPS`: 5-year note, Apr/Oct). That rule was falsified twice over: cross-checking CNBC's historical yields against real TreasuryDirect FedInvest prices (the same historical-price tool at `treasurydirect.gov/GA-FI/FedInvest/selectSecurityPriceDate`, computing each candidate bond's real yield from its FedInvest price via `shared/src/bond-math.js` and comparing to what CNBC actually reported that day) first showed a **lag of several weeks that varies cycle to cycle** after each Jan15/Jul15 (or Apr15/Oct15) checkpoint, and later — extending the log further back — showed `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS` sometimes match a bond from the *other* origin-auction family altogether (5-year-origin Apr15/Oct15) rather than only ever the 10-year-origin Jan15/Jul15 family. No fixed rule reproduces either behavior, so none is used: rollover dates are pinned individually by a broad cross-check (testing every plausible TIPS maturity, any family, against what CNBC actually reported that day) and recorded as they're found. Some transitions resolve through more than one intermediate cohort in quick succession rather than a single clean flip — e.g. `US1YTIPS` matched three different maturities within about six weeks in Feb 2025 — each is recorded as its own entry.

**Price selection**: FedInvest's `sell` price (what a holder would receive — the bid) is used for each candidate, falling back to `buy` (the ask) when a candidate has no active bid that day (`sell=0`) — CNBC's own quotes are bid-side, so `sell` is the correct proxy, not `buy`. (An earlier version of this had the two backwards — `buy`-primary — which silently corrupted several pinned dates: it manufactured a brief intermediate cohort in Feb 2025 that doesn't survive the corrected price, and shifted two other boundary dates by a few days; see the corrected table below.) The raw `eod` evaluated price is not used at all: it was found to produce a stale, misleading price on at least one no-bid day.

**Issue-date fallback (a second, lower-confidence pinning method)**: `US10YTIPS` and part of `US30YTIPS`'s history are structurally unresolvable by the cross-check above — adjacent cohorts' real yields sit within ~1-2bp of each other, below the cross-check's own noise floor, for reasons unrelated to data quality (see `2.2_SAO_Residual_Analysis.md`: the seasonal effect itself is what's amortizing away at these maturities, so there's less signal to distinguish cohorts by in the first place). This is *not* a reversion to the falsified calendar-rule assumption: the cross-check fails here specifically *because* the candidates are close, which means the flip date barely matters — the SA error from picking the "wrong" day is bounded by that same sub-1-2bp gap, not by the tens of bp the falsified calendar rules were wrong by. For these stretches, the flip date is instead derived from the bond's real issuance timing: **the first trading day strictly after the last business day of the month containing the new cohort's `TipsRef.csv` `datedDate`** (10-Year cohorts: Jan/Jul; 30-Year: Feb). This rule reproduces the one empirically cross-checked `US30YTIPS` transition exactly (last business day of Feb 2026 was Fri 2026-02-27, still the prior cohort; the new cohort's first observed date was 2026-03-02, the next trading day) and independently matches the cross-checked `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS` 2025-08-01 entries above (same rule, same July-2025 cohort, computed from `TipsRef.csv` alone with no yield data involved).

`SA_ROLLOVER_LOG` entries are `{ from, maturity }`, ascending by `from` (ET date): the maturity in effect from that date until the next entry (or indefinitely, for the last one). A trade date before a symbol's earliest entry has no observed bond identity and is left as a gap on the SA line — never guessed; so is any stretch where candidate bonds' implied yields sit too close together to disambiguate reliably (a flat 1-2yr TIPS curve), even between two dated entries — represented as `{ from, maturity: null }`, since no real TIPS maturity is ever `null`. ## The transform, per point
For each historical/current yield quote:
1. `settle` = the quote's trade date, advanced to the next SIFMA bond-trading day (T+1 — see DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date; holidays from `misc/BondHolidaysSifma.csv`).
2. `mature`/`coupon` = resolved per the bond-identity rule above.
3. `price = priceFromYield(quotedYield, coupon, settle, mature)` (shared/src/bond-math.js — standard Excel `YIELD`-convention bond math, frequency=2, actual/actual; see TIPS_Basics.md).
4. `saPrice = price × (SA_Factor(settle) / SA_Factor(mature))` (SA factors from `TIPS/RefCpiNsaSa.csv`, looked up via `shared/src/ref-cpi.js#saFactorForDate` — exact date if published, else the most recent past occurrence of the same calendar month/day, since the daily SA factor is a slowly-drifting annual pattern and no future values are published).
5. `saYield = yieldFromPrice(saPrice, coupon, settle, mature)`.

## UI
- **Toggle**: single "Show SA Yield (TIPS)" checkbox in the sidebar — drives the overlay on all three tabs (Time Series, Yield Curves, Breakeven Inflation) at once, not a per-tab setting. Lazily fetches `RefCpiNsaSa.csv`, `TipsRef.csv`, `BondHolidaysSifma.csv`, and the CNBC bond-metadata endpoint on first use (not on page load) — cached for the session; bond metadata is refreshed on "Refresh Data".
- **Time Series overlay**: a solid amber (`#f59e0b`) second line per eligible chart (not dashed — distinguishing color, not line style, per user preference). Tooltip labels it "SA Yield" vs. "Yield" for the raw series. `US10YTIPS`/`US30YTIPS` plot back through their issue-date-fallback coverage (see rollover log table) — lower-confidence than the other three symbols' cross-checked lines, but the error is small by construction (see Known limitations).
- **Yield Curves / BEI overlay**: two amber datasets (dashed Start / solid End, matching the raw curve's blue/red dash convention) at all five TIPS maturities — see [2.2_Yield_Curves.md](./2.2_Yield_Curves.md#sa-overlay) and [2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md](./2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md#sa-overlay). The Start point breaks (gap) for any maturity/date the rollover log can't resolve; the End point still resolves whenever the active range's end date is today, since today's bond identity always comes from the live CNBC quote, not the log.
- **Legend**: a small amber-swatch legend line appears under the checkbox while SA is on, clarifying what the color means.
- **Sidebar**: an additional reading next to each eligible symbol's yield/change (today's SA yield, no "SA" prefix — color and the legend already identify it).
- **Y-axis auto-rescale**: `rescaleYToVisible` includes the SA dataset's values (not just the raw series) when computing visible Y bounds, and toggling the checkbox forces a rescale bypassing any prior manual Y-zoom — otherwise the SA line (which can differ from raw by tens of bps) can render off-canvas or clipped. This bit the feature during development: it looked like "no data" in 2D and "clipped below axis" in 10D, but was actually a Y-bounds bug, not a data bug.

## Known limitations
- **US5YTIPS feed reliability**: the underlying CNBC feed for this symbol is flaky with large gaps (see [1.0_Operation.md](./1.0_Operation.md) symbol-reliability note) — SA is computed for it the same way as 1Y/2Y, but it isn't a priority for correctness/robustness work given the feed itself is the limiting factor.
- **US5YTIPS rollover-log gap pre-2026-03-20**: not a missing-data problem — deep history exists back to 2004 — but candidate bonds' implied yields sit too close together in that period to reliably identify which one CNBC was quoting, unlike the 1Y/2Y case where the same broad cross-check does resolve most stretches back to 2024-03-01.
- **US10YTIPS has no empirically cross-checked coverage**: structurally unresolvable by the yield cross-check (see rollover log narrative) — all of its `SA_ROLLOVER_LOG` coverage, and part of `US30YTIPS`'s (pre-2026-03-02), comes from the lower-confidence issue-date fallback instead. This is a deliberate, bounded-error tradeoff, not an oversight — see the rollover log narrative for why the flip date barely matters here.
- **US10YTIPS/US30YTIPS SA effect is small by construction**: the seasonal effect amortizes with maturity (YieldCurves/knowledge/2.2_SAO_Residual_Analysis.md finds it's at or below ~1-2bp noise level beyond roughly 6 years to maturity) — expect the SA line at these maturities to sit almost exactly on top of the raw yield line. That's the correct, expected result, not a rendering bug.
- **Coverage boundary**: if a trade date's resolved `targetMaturity` isn't in `TipsRef.csv` (predates the reference data, or a future checkpoint's target hasn't been auctioned yet), that point is skipped (gap in the SA line) rather than guessed.
