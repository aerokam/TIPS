# 2.4 Seasonal Adjustment (SA) Overlay

## Dependencies
[YieldCurves/knowledge/1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md](../../YieldCurves/knowledge/1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) (the SA transform itself — foundational, read first), [YieldCurves/knowledge/2.1_SA_Intuition.md](../../YieldCurves/knowledge/2.1_SA_Intuition.md), [1.0_Operation.md](./1.0_Operation.md) (symbol reliability), [2.1_Time_Series.md](./2.1_Time_Series.md), [2.2_Yield_Curves.md](./2.2_Yield_Curves.md), [2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md](./2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md), [DATA_DICTIONARY.md](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield), [TIPS_Basics.md](../../knowledge/TIPS_Basics.md) (yield calculation conventions).

## Purpose
Adds an optional Seasonally Adjusted (SA) yield overlay to the Time Series tab's TIPS charts, the Yield Curves tab's TIPS curve, and the Breakeven Inflation tab's BEI curve, for the maturities where the seasonal effect is large enough to matter: **1Y, 2Y, 5Y**. SA is not offered for 10Y/30Y — the effect amortizes with maturity and is negligible there (see YieldCurves/knowledge/2.2_SAO_Residual_Analysis.md).

## The key fact this feature depends on
CNBC's `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS`/`US5YTIPS` symbols are **not** synthetic constant-maturity indices — each is the bid yield of one specific, real TIPS. CNBC's own quote page identifies which one (e.g. `US2YTIPS` = the Jan 2028 0.50% TIPS as of 2026-07-01). This means the exact same Price → SA Price → SA Yield transform YieldCurves applies to actual TIPS applies here directly, using that bond's real coupon and maturity date — no synthetic-bond construction is needed or used.

## Bond identity: today vs. history
- **Today's point**: uses CNBC's live quote REST endpoint (`quote.cnbc.com/quote-html-webservice/restQuote/symbolType/symbol`, batched with `|`-separated symbols) for `maturity_date` and `coupon` directly — empirically confirmed to match the actual quoted price (`bond_last_price`) to the cent.
- **Every other (historical) point**: CNBC only exposes each symbol's *current* underlying bond, not a historical CUSIP-per-date mapping. The bond identity instead comes from `SA_ROLLOVER_LOG` (`src/app.js`) — an observed record of which maturity each symbol actually quoted on which dates — joined against `TipsRef.csv` (S2, the full TIPS auction record) for that maturity's coupon.

## The rollover log
An earlier version of this feature assumed CNBC re-picks the underlying bond on a fixed calendar rule — every 6 months, on the 15th of the tenor's origin-auction month (`US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS`: 10-year note, Jan/Jul; `US5YTIPS`: 5-year note, Apr/Oct). That rule was falsified: cross-checking CNBC's historical yields against real TreasuryDirect FedInvest prices (the same historical-price tool at `treasurydirect.gov/GA-FI/FedInvest/selectSecurityPriceDate`, computing each candidate bond's real yield from its FedInvest price via `shared/src/bond-math.js` and comparing to what CNBC actually reported that day) showed CNBC does roll on that Jan15/Jul15 (or Apr15/Oct15) cadence, but only after a **lag of several weeks that varies cycle to cycle** — one cycle rolled ~3 weeks after its checkpoint, the next was still not rolled 5+ weeks after its checkpoint (as of 2026-08-18). No fixed offset reproduces this, so no formula is used at all: rollover dates are pinned individually by that same cross-check method (bisecting candidate dates until one candidate bond's implied yield stops matching CNBC's reported yield and the other starts) and recorded as they're found.

`SA_ROLLOVER_LOG` entries are `{ from, maturity }`, ascending by `from` (ET date): the maturity in effect from that date until the next entry (or indefinitely, for the last one). A trade date before a symbol's earliest entry has no observed bond identity and is left as a gap on the SA line — never guessed. Pinned so far:

| Symbol | From | Maturity | How pinned |
|---|---|---|---|
| `US1YTIPS` | 2025-08-04 | 2026-07-15 | Bisected between 2025-07-28 (still prior cohort) and 2025-08-04 |
| `US1YTIPS` | 2026-02-10 | 2027-01-15 | Bisected between 2026-02-03 (still prior cohort) and 2026-02-10 |
| `US2YTIPS` | 2025-08-04 | 2027-07-15 | Same rollover event as `US1YTIPS` above — 1Y/2Y roll together |
| `US2YTIPS` | 2026-02-10 | 2028-01-15 | Same rollover event as `US1YTIPS` above |
| `US5YTIPS` | 2026-03-20 | 2030-10-15 | Earliest date cross-checked (CNBC's `US5YTIPS` feed has no data at all before this — see Known limitations); earlier points are a gap |
| `US5YTIPS` | 2026-06-01 | 2031-04-15 | CNBC's feed has no data between 2026-04-01 (last confirmed on prior maturity) and 2026-06-01 (first confirmed on this one) — exact rollover date unpinnable, but moot since there's no raw yield to adjust in that gap either |

When a new rollover is suspected (e.g. `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS` are overdue for a roll off `2027-01-15`/`2028-01-15` as of 2026-08-18 — the Jul 15, 2026 checkpoint has already passed), extend the table using the same cross-check method rather than assuming a date.

## The transform, per point
For each historical/current yield quote:
1. `settle` = the quote's trade date, advanced to the next SIFMA bond-trading day (T+1 — see DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date; holidays from `misc/BondHolidaysSifma.csv`).
2. `mature`/`coupon` = resolved per the bond-identity rule above.
3. `price = priceFromYield(quotedYield, coupon, settle, mature)` (shared/src/bond-math.js — standard Excel `YIELD`-convention bond math, frequency=2, actual/actual; see TIPS_Basics.md).
4. `saPrice = price × (SA_Factor(settle) / SA_Factor(mature))` (SA factors from `TIPS/RefCpiNsaSa.csv`, looked up via `shared/src/ref-cpi.js#saFactorForDate` — exact date if published, else the most recent past occurrence of the same calendar month/day, since the daily SA factor is a slowly-drifting annual pattern and no future values are published).
5. `saYield = yieldFromPrice(saPrice, coupon, settle, mature)`.

## UI
- **Toggle**: single "Show SA Yield (1Y/2Y/5Y TIPS)" checkbox in the sidebar — drives the overlay on all three tabs (Time Series, Yield Curves, Breakeven Inflation) at once, not a per-tab setting. Lazily fetches `RefCpiNsaSa.csv`, `TipsRef.csv`, `BondHolidaysSifma.csv`, and the CNBC bond-metadata endpoint on first use (not on page load) — cached for the session; bond metadata is refreshed on "Refresh Data".
- **Time Series overlay**: a solid amber (`#f59e0b`) second line per eligible chart (not dashed — distinguishing color, not line style, per user preference). Tooltip labels it "SA Yield" vs. "Yield" for the raw series.
- **Yield Curves / BEI overlay**: two amber datasets (dashed Start / solid End, matching the raw curve's blue/red dash convention) at the 1Y/2Y/5Y maturities only — see [2.2_Yield_Curves.md](./2.2_Yield_Curves.md#sa-overlay) and [2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md](./2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md#sa-overlay).
- **Legend**: a small amber-swatch legend line appears under the checkbox while SA is on, clarifying what the color means.
- **Sidebar**: an additional reading next to each eligible symbol's yield/change (today's SA yield, no "SA" prefix — color and the legend already identify it).
- **Y-axis auto-rescale**: `rescaleYToVisible` includes the SA dataset's values (not just the raw series) when computing visible Y bounds, and toggling the checkbox forces a rescale bypassing any prior manual Y-zoom — otherwise the SA line (which can differ from raw by tens of bps) can render off-canvas or clipped. This bit the feature during development: it looked like "no data" in 2D and "clipped below axis" in 10D, but was actually a Y-bounds bug, not a data bug.

## Known limitations
- **US5YTIPS feed reliability**: the underlying CNBC feed for this symbol is flaky with large gaps (see [1.0_Operation.md](./1.0_Operation.md) symbol-reliability note) — SA is computed for it the same way as 1Y/2Y, but it isn't a priority for correctness/robustness work given the feed itself is the limiting factor.
- **Coverage boundary**: if a trade date's resolved `targetMaturity` isn't in `TipsRef.csv` (predates the reference data, or a future checkpoint's target hasn't been auctioned yet), that point is skipped (gap in the SA line) rather than guessed.
