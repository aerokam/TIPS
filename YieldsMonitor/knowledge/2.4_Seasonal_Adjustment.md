# 2.4 Seasonal Adjustment (SA) Overlay

## Dependencies
[YieldCurves/knowledge/1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md](../../YieldCurves/knowledge/1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) (the SA transform itself — foundational, read first), [YieldCurves/knowledge/2.1_SA_Intuition.md](../../YieldCurves/knowledge/2.1_SA_Intuition.md), [1.0_Operation.md](./1.0_Operation.md) (symbol reliability), [2.1_Time_Series.md](./2.1_Time_Series.md), [2.2_Yield_Curves.md](./2.2_Yield_Curves.md), [2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md](./2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md), [DATA_DICTIONARY.md](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield), [TIPS_Basics.md](../../knowledge/TIPS_Basics.md) (yield calculation conventions).

## Purpose
Adds an optional Seasonally Adjusted (SA) yield overlay to the Time Series tab's TIPS charts, the Yield Curves tab's TIPS curve, and the Breakeven Inflation tab's BEI curve, for all five TIPS maturities the app tracks: **1Y, 2Y, 5Y, 10Y, 30Y**. The seasonal effect amortizes with maturity and is at or below noise level by 10Y/30Y (see YieldCurves/knowledge/2.2_SAO_Residual_Analysis.md) — the SA line there sits almost on top of the raw line — but it's still computed and shown at those maturities for curve completeness, rather than leaving the Yield Curves/BEI overlays visibly stop at 5Y.

## The key fact this feature depends on
CNBC's `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS`/`US5YTIPS`/`US10YTIPS`/`US30YTIPS` symbols are **not** synthetic constant-maturity indices — each is the bid yield of one specific, real TIPS. CNBC's own quote page identifies which one (e.g. `US2YTIPS` = the Jan 2028 0.50% TIPS as of 2026-07-01). This means the exact same Price → SA Price → SA Yield transform YieldCurves applies to actual TIPS applies here directly, using that bond's real coupon and maturity date — no synthetic-bond construction is needed or used.

## Bond identity: today vs. history
- **Today's point**: uses CNBC's live quote REST endpoint (`quote.cnbc.com/quote-html-webservice/restQuote/symbolType/symbol`, batched with `|`-separated symbols) for `maturity_date` and `coupon` directly — empirically confirmed to match the actual quoted price (`bond_last_price`) to the cent.
- **Every other (historical) point**: CNBC only exposes each symbol's *current* underlying bond, not a historical CUSIP-per-date mapping. The bond identity instead comes from `SA_ROLLOVER_LOG` (`src/app.js`) — an observed record of which maturity each symbol actually quoted on which dates — joined against `TipsRef.csv` (S2, the full TIPS auction record) for that maturity's coupon.

## The rollover log
An earlier version of this feature assumed CNBC re-picks the underlying bond on a fixed calendar rule — every 6 months, on the 15th of the tenor's origin-auction month, always the same family (`US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS`: 10-year note, Jan/Jul; `US5YTIPS`: 5-year note, Apr/Oct). That rule was falsified twice over: cross-checking CNBC's historical yields against real TreasuryDirect FedInvest prices (the same historical-price tool at `treasurydirect.gov/GA-FI/FedInvest/selectSecurityPriceDate`, computing each candidate bond's real yield from its FedInvest price via `shared/src/bond-math.js` and comparing to what CNBC actually reported that day) first showed a **lag of several weeks that varies cycle to cycle** after each Jan15/Jul15 (or Apr15/Oct15) checkpoint, and later — extending the log further back — showed `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS` sometimes match a bond from the *other* origin-auction family altogether (5-year-origin Apr15/Oct15) rather than only ever the 10-year-origin Jan15/Jul15 family. No fixed rule reproduces either behavior, so none is used: rollover dates are pinned individually by a broad cross-check (testing every plausible TIPS maturity, any family, against what CNBC actually reported that day) and recorded as they're found. Some transitions resolve through more than one intermediate cohort in quick succession rather than a single clean flip — e.g. `US1YTIPS` matched three different maturities within about six weeks in Feb 2025 — each is recorded as its own entry.

**Price selection**: FedInvest's `sell` price (what a holder would receive — the bid) is used for each candidate, falling back to `buy` (the ask) when a candidate has no active bid that day (`sell=0`) — CNBC's own quotes are bid-side, so `sell` is the correct proxy, not `buy`. (An earlier version of this had the two backwards — `buy`-primary — which silently corrupted several pinned dates: it manufactured a brief intermediate cohort in Feb 2025 that doesn't survive the corrected price, and shifted two other boundary dates by a few days; see the corrected table below.) The raw `eod` evaluated price is not used at all: it was found to produce a stale, misleading price on at least one no-bid day.

**Issue-date fallback (a second, lower-confidence pinning method)**: `US5YTIPS`, `US10YTIPS`, and part of `US30YTIPS`'s history are structurally unresolvable by the cross-check above — adjacent cohorts' real yields sit within ~1-2bp of each other, below the cross-check's own noise floor, for reasons unrelated to data quality (see `2.2_SAO_Residual_Analysis.md`: the seasonal effect itself is what's amortizing away at these maturities, so there's less signal to distinguish cohorts by in the first place). This is *not* a reversion to the falsified calendar-rule assumption: the cross-check fails here specifically *because* the candidates are close, which means the flip date barely matters — the SA error from picking the "wrong" day is bounded by that same sub-1-2bp gap, not by the tens of bp the falsified calendar rules were wrong by. For these stretches, the flip date is instead derived from the bond's real issuance timing: **the first trading day strictly after the last business day of the month containing the new cohort's `TipsRef.csv` `datedDate`** (5-Year cohorts: Apr/Oct; 10-Year: Jan/Jul; 30-Year: Feb). This rule reproduces the one empirically cross-checked `US30YTIPS` transition exactly (last business day of Feb 2026 was Fri 2026-02-27, still the prior cohort; the new cohort's first observed date was 2026-03-02, the next trading day) and independently matches the cross-checked `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS` 2025-08-01 entries above (same rule, same July-2025 cohort, computed from `TipsRef.csv` alone with no yield data involved).

**Closest-remaining-maturity fallback (a third pinning method, used only for `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS` before their earliest cross-checked entry)**: the issue-date fallback above doesn't transfer here — it identifies which bond is *newly issued* in a given month, but `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS` track a bond *nearing* maturity, not a new one, and (per the rollover-log narrative above) which real-world family it's drawn from varies unpredictably. Instead: for a given date, among every `TipsRef.csv` bond already issued and not yet matured as of that date, pick whichever one's maturity is closest to (date + 1 year) for `US1YTIPS`, or (date + 2 years) for `US2YTIPS`; walk forward and record a new entry each time that pick changes. This reproduces the observed rollover cadence (rolling to the next cohort roughly every quarter, drawing from whichever bond — 5-year-origin or 10-year-origin — happens to have the nearest maturity) without needing to resolve which real-world family is in effect on any given date, which the empirical cross-check itself cannot do reliably this far back. As with the issue-date fallback, this is a deliberate precision-for-coverage tradeoff: it is not a claim that CNBC's quote actually tracked this exact bond on these dates, only that the resulting SA value is a reasonable estimate given no quoted value should be left without one.

`SA_ROLLOVER_LOG` entries are `{ from, maturity }`, ascending by `from` (ET date): the maturity in effect from that date until the next entry (or indefinitely, for the last one). Per explicit direction: a quoted yield with no SA counterpart at all is worse than a lower-confidence SA value, so a trade date before a symbol's earliest cross-checked entry, or inside a stretch where candidate bonds' implied yields sit too close together to disambiguate reliably (a flat 1-2yr TIPS curve), is filled by a fallback method (closest-remaining-maturity for `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS`, issue-date for `US5YTIPS`/`US10YTIPS`/`US30YTIPS` — both described below) rather than left as a gap. A gap (`{ from, maturity: null }`, since no real TIPS maturity is ever `null`) is reserved only for a genuine absence of any plausible candidate bond (an issuance hiatus — see Known limitations), not mere ambiguity between real candidates. ## The transform, per point
For each historical/current yield quote:
1. `settle` = the quote's trade date, advanced to the next SIFMA bond-trading day (T+1 — see DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date; holidays from `misc/BondHolidaysSifma.csv`).
2. `mature`/`coupon` = resolved per the bond-identity rule above.
3. `price = priceFromYield(quotedYield, coupon, settle, mature)` (shared/src/bond-math.js — standard Excel `YIELD`-convention bond math, frequency=2, actual/actual; see TIPS_Basics.md).
4. `saPrice = price × (SA_Factor(settle) / SA_Factor(mature))` (SA factors from `TIPS/RefCpiNsaSa.csv`, looked up via `shared/src/ref-cpi.js#saFactorForDate` — exact date if published, else the most recent past occurrence of the same calendar month/day, since the daily SA factor is a slowly-drifting annual pattern and no future values are published).
5. `saYield = yieldFromPrice(saPrice, coupon, settle, mature)`.

## UI
- **Toggle**: "Show TIPS yields" in the sidebar, two checkboxes — "Quoted" and "Seasonally Adjusted (SA)" — both checked by default. Either can be unchecked on its own, but not both at once: unchecking the second-remaining checkbox re-checks itself instead of leaving neither selected. Drives both the raw (quoted) and SA lines on all three tabs (Time Series, Yield Curves, Breakeven Inflation) at once, not a per-tab setting. SA prerequisites (`RefCpiNsaSa.csv`, `TipsRef.csv`, `BondHolidaysSifma.csv`, the CNBC bond-metadata endpoint) load on page load since SA is on by default, in parallel with the initial data fetch; bond metadata is refreshed on "Refresh Data".
- **Time Series overlay**: raw yield as the first line per eligible chart; SA yield as a solid amber (`#f59e0b`) second line (not dashed — distinguishing color, not line style, per user preference). Tooltip labels it "SA Yield" vs. "Yield" for the raw series. Unchecking "Quoted" or "SA" empties that chart's corresponding dataset rather than removing the chart. `US5YTIPS`/`US10YTIPS`/`US30YTIPS` plot back through their issue-date-fallback coverage (see rollover log table) — lower-confidence than `US1YTIPS`/`US2YTIPS`'s cross-checked lines, but the error is small by construction for `US10YTIPS`/`US30YTIPS` (see Known limitations); `US5YTIPS`'s feed is separately unreliable regardless (see 1.0_Operation.md symbol-reliability note).
- **Yield Curves / BEI overlay**: raw Start/End (blue dashed/red solid) and SA Start/End (amber dashed/solid) datasets at all five TIPS maturities — see [2.2_Yield_Curves.md](./2.2_Yield_Curves.md#sa-overlay) and [2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md](./2.3_Breakeven_Inflation.md#sa-overlay). Unchecking "Quoted" empties the raw Start/End datasets (and, on the BEI curve, the raw breakeven line, since every BEI pair has a TIPS leg); unchecking "SA" empties the SA counterparts. This only applies to the TIPS curve and the BEI curve — the Nominal curve has no SA counterpart and always shows its Start/End lines regardless of either checkbox. The chart's own legend (native to these two tabs, not part of the sidebar toggle) hides an entry when its corresponding checkbox is off. A Start point still breaks (gap) for any maturity/date the rollover log can't resolve; the End point still resolves whenever the active range's end date is today, since today's bond identity always comes from the live CNBC quote, not the log.
- **Sidebar**: an additional reading next to each eligible symbol's yield/change (today's SA yield, no "SA" prefix — color already identifies it) — unaffected by either checkbox, since it's a numeric summary rather than a plotted line.
- **Y-axis auto-rescale**: `rescaleYToVisible` includes the SA dataset's values (not just the raw series) when computing visible Y bounds, and toggling either checkbox forces a rescale bypassing any prior manual Y-zoom — otherwise a line (which can differ from the other by tens of bps) can render off-canvas or clipped, or a newly-emptied line can leave the axis scaled to nothing visible. This bit the feature during development: it looked like "no data" in 2D and "clipped below axis" in 10D, but was actually a Y-bounds bug, not a data bug.

## Known limitations
- **US5YTIPS feed reliability**: the underlying CNBC feed for this symbol is flaky with large gaps (see [1.0_Operation.md](./1.0_Operation.md) symbol-reliability note) — SA is computed for it the same way as 1Y/2Y, but it isn't a priority for correctness/robustness work given the feed itself is the limiting factor.
- **US5YTIPS rollover-log gap pre-2006-04-15**: not a missing-data problem for the whole stretch — CNBC's deep history exists back to 2004 — but 5-Year TIPS themselves had an issuance hiatus (last pre-2004 cohort dated 1997-07-15, maturing 2002-07-15; no cohort again until 2006-04-15), so a quote in that dead stretch isn't a real on-the-run 5-Year TIPS and is left an explicit gap, same reasoning as `US30YTIPS`'s 2001-2010 hiatus below.
- **US5YTIPS/US10YTIPS have no empirically cross-checked coverage** (aside from `US5YTIPS`'s two most recent entries): structurally unresolvable by the yield cross-check (see rollover log narrative) — nearly all of `US5YTIPS`'s and `US10YTIPS`'s `SA_ROLLOVER_LOG` coverage, and part of `US30YTIPS`'s (pre-2026-03-02), comes from the lower-confidence issue-date fallback instead. This is a deliberate, bounded-error tradeoff, not an oversight — see the rollover log narrative for why the flip date barely matters here.
- **US1YTIPS/US2YTIPS's 2014-04-01..2024-02-29 coverage is lower-confidence by construction**: the closest-remaining-maturity fallback (see rollover log narrative) picks a real, plausible bond for every date in this range, but — unlike the issue-date fallback's bounded-error argument for 5Y/10Y/30Y — the candidates here are not necessarily close to each other in yield, since which real-world family (5-year-origin vs. 10-year-origin) CNBC actually tracked on a given date is genuinely unresolved. The SA value in this range is a reasonable estimate, not a cross-checked one.
- **US10YTIPS/US30YTIPS SA effect is small by construction**: the seasonal effect amortizes with maturity (YieldCurves/knowledge/2.2_SAO_Residual_Analysis.md finds it's at or below ~1-2bp noise level beyond roughly 6 years to maturity) — expect the SA line at these maturities to sit almost exactly on top of the raw yield line. That's the correct, expected result, not a rendering bug.
- **Coverage boundary**: if a trade date's resolved `targetMaturity` isn't in `TipsRef.csv` at all (predates the reference data, or a future checkpoint's target hasn't been auctioned yet), that point is skipped (gap in the SA line) rather than guessed — this is the genuine-absence case the fallback methods above don't try to paper over.
